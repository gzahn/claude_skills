# Culture and Isolation Media Recipes

> Source: Foster, Bills & Mueller (2004) *Biodiversity of Fungi: Inventory and Monitoring Methods*. Academic Press. Appendix II: Formulae for Selected Materials Used to Isolate and Study Fungi and Fungal Allies.

---

## General Preparation Notes

All dry ingredients and agar are combined and heated with stirring until fully dissolved. Fill flasks or bottles to about half capacity (boiling over during autoclaving is common with oatmeal- and starch-based media). Autoclave 15–20 minutes at 121°C (15 psi); extend to 30–45 minutes for viscous or dirty natural materials (soil, oatmeal). Heat-sensitive components (vitamins, antibiotics) are added after autoclaving but before the medium solidifies (~45–50°C). Increase agar by ~5 g/L in very warm climates or when an especially firm medium is needed.

### A/T OOMYCETE MEDIUM
*(modified from Newall & Fell 1994)*

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| V8 juice | 20.0 ml |
| Amphotericin-B | 2.5 mg |
| Thiabendazole | 10.0 mg |
| Chloramphenicol | 10.0 mg |
| Ampicillin | 250.0 mg |
| Rifampicin | 10.0 mg |
| Agar | 20.0 g |
| Dilute seawater (15 g salts/liter) | 1,000.0 ml |

**Preparation:** Bring seawater, V8 juice, and agar to boil. Autoclave 20 minutes. Cool to 40–50°C. Add inhibitors as mixed powder; agitate thoroughly. Some ingredients are highly toxic — use lab safety apparel and gloves.

**Use:** Particularly effective for selecting *Halophytophthora* species from submerged organic debris. Selective for marine/estuarine Oomycetes.

|---|---|

| Agar | 15.0 g |

## Antibiotics Reference

### Antibacterial Antibiotics

Critical notes: Most antibacterial antibiotics are heat-sensitive — prepare as concentrated stock solutions in sterile water or filter-sterilize, then add to cooled media (45–50°C). **Exception: chloramphenicol** can be autoclaved; typically used at 100 mg/L.

Common antibacterial combinations:
- **General surveys of filamentous soil fungi:** chlortetracycline 50 mg/L + streptomycin sulfate 50 mg/L
- **Rose bengal** (not an antibiotic) also inhibits many bacteria and reduces colony expansion; incubate plates in dark (photosensitizer)
- **Acidification to pH 4.5** with lactic acid also limits bacterial growth

| Antibiotic | Preparation | Final concentration |
|---|---|---|
| Chloramphenicol | 95% ethanol | 100 mg/L |
| Gentamicin | Distilled water | 50 mg/L |
| Streptomycin sulfate | Distilled water | 50 mg/L |
| Tetracycline or chlortetracycline | Distilled water | 50 mg/L |

**Caution:** Some antibacterial antibiotics may be selectively toxic to Oomycetes and basidiomycetes. If selective toxicity is suspected, substitute other formulations.

### Mite Control — Dieldrin Medium Supplement

*Last resort only* — when mites have infested or threaten to destroy cultures:

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Dieldrin | 200.0 mg |
| Acetone | 10.0 ml |

Add 1 ml of acetone stock to medium before or after autoclaving (final: 20 mg/ml). Dieldrin is **extremely toxic to animals** — avoid inhalation and skin contact. Handle all glassware and media as toxic waste. Alternatively: lindane at 75 mg/ml (Gams et al. 1998).

## Fixatives

### 2X CTAB Buffer (DNA preservation in fresh fungal tissue)

Per 100-ml batch: Tris-HCl 1.21 g, NaCl 8.182 g, EDTA 74.0 mg, CTAB 2.0 g, polyvinylpyrolidone 2.0 ml, 2-mercaptoethanol 0.2 ml, distilled water to 100 ml. Shelf life 2–3 months; dispense 500-ml aliquots into sterile vials. Fresh fungal tissue (up to 200–300 mg) placed directly into 500 µl buffer.

### DMSO, Supersaturated Solution (DNA preservation)
*(Wu et al. 2000)*

DMSO 20.0 ml, EDTA 8.4 g, NaCl ~36.0 g (to saturation), distilled water to 100 ml. Yields 20% DMSO + 250 mM EDTA in saturated NaCl. Prevents DNA degradation during long-term storage.

### Neutral Buffered Formalin

Formalin (37% aqueous formaldehyde) 100.0 ml + distilled water 900.0 ml + CaCO₃ or MgCO₃ to saturation.

### FAA (Formalin-Acetic Acid-Alcohol)

Formalin 5.0 ml + glacial acetic acid 5.0 ml + ethanol (50%) 90.0 ml.
