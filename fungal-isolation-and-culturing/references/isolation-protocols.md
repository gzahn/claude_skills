# Isolation Protocols — General Methods by Sample Type

> Source: Foster, Bills & Mueller (2004) *Biodiversity of Fungi: Inventory and Monitoring Methods*. Academic Press. Primarily Ch. 13 (Saprobic Soil Fungi — Bills et al.) with cross-references to other chapters.

---

## Soil Fungi — Saprobic Filamentous Fungi

### Collection and Storage of Soil

1. Remove litter and humus from ~20 cm² area
2. Sterilize digging instrument (trowel, knife, spatula, spoon, coring tube) in 70% ethanol and dry
3. Collect 6–10 plugs (10–30 g) from exposed soil surface; place in labeled, sterile bags
4. Collect from A1 horizon (upper mineral soil) for most surveys; also include litter/humus layers in protocol if relevant
5. Process samples promptly; store at 4°C if delay necessary

### Method 1: Suspension Plating (Quantitative Standard)

**Best for:** General quantitative surveys; most filamentous ascomycetes and mitosporic fungi; Zygomycetes.

**Protocol:**
1. Prepare soil suspension: dilute soil in sterile water or 0.05–0.2% agar / 0.1–0.2% dextrin or carboxymethylcellulose solution (retards sedimentation)
2. **Determine appropriate soil:water ratio by trial** to yield 5–30 colonies per 100-mm Petri plate (low density essential to minimize intercolony interaction)
3. Pipette diluted soil suspension onto agar plates; OR mix aliquot with cooled agar and pour plates
4. Incubate at 17–24°C or at in situ temperature first, then at room temperature
5. **Recommended minimum:** 180–250 isolates per site for quantitative comparative studies; 600–1000 isolates per site for comprehensive inventory

**Media:** Use nutrient-poor isolation media (see media-recipes.md: DRBC, MEA at low concentration, GAN). Include colony-restricting agents (rose bengal, cyclosporin A, dichloran) to limit fast-growing fungi and allow recovery of slower species.

### Method 2: Warcup Soil Plates (Simple/Rapid)

**Best for:** Preliminary assessments; basidiomycetes; rapid processing.

**Protocol:**
1. Disperse minute quantities (0.2–5.0 mg) of finely pulverized soil across bottom of sterile Petri dish
2. Pour cooled (but still liquid) agar over soil
3. Agitate gently to disperse particles
4. Incubate as above

**Alternative (Drechsler's method):** Sprinkle minute quantities of fresh soil or humus directly on water agar or very dilute cornmeal agar — creates microcosms; favors infection/sporulation of parasitic fungi and isolation of fast-growing oomycetes from soil, litter, and root fragments.

**Advantage over suspension plating:** Yields basidiomycetes not recovered by suspension plating; simpler.

### Method 3: Particle Filtration ("Soil Washing")

**Best for:** Mycelial fragments in substrata; rare and slow-growing species; litter fungi; aquatic litter; decaying wood; coprophilous fungi; fungi with chlamydospores in organic particles.

**Principle:** Washes soil/substratum through nested sieves to produce particles of specific size range; plates separated particles on isolation media. Favors fungi growing as mycelia vs. spore-forming fungi in suspension plating.

**Protocol:**
1. Wash soil through nested sieves to achieve desired particle size range (typically 50–80 µm; or 105–210 µm for forest litter)
2. Plate constant volume of washed particles on isolation media
3. Count and identify resulting colonies
4. Optional: separate soil into microcomponents (organic vs. mineral) before plating for microhabitat preference data

**Key equipment:** Washing devices range from simple nested sieves to complex self-flushing devices; choice of device is a matter of personal preference and resource availability.

### Fractionation Techniques (Selective/Enrichment)

**Ethanol pasteurization (for ascosporic fungi):**
1. Treat soil with 95% ethanol for specified time before plating
2. Dramatically increases recovery of ascomycetes (especially Eurotiales, Chaetomium, Xylaria, coprophilous fungi, ascosporic yeasts)
3. Reduces mitosporic fungi and yeasts with thin-walled conidia
4. Ethanol treatment alone produces same results as ethanol + mild heat treatment

**High-temperature incubation:** Incubate isolation plates at elevated temperatures to select thermotolerant/thermophilic species (see taxon-specific/others.md for extremophile protocols).

**Density-gradient centrifugation:** For large ascospores (sucrose gradient; Paden 1967) or basidiospores/sclerotia in soil (Percoll gradient; Okuda et al. 1995). Preferable for concentrating specific propagule types before plating.

**Selective media for basidiomycetes:** LGB agar (lignin-guaiacol-benomyl; Thorn et al. 1996) — use after particle filtration to remove most mitosporic fungi; guaiacol acts as colorimetric indicator of lignin-modifying enzymes. See media-recipes.md.

### Media Selection for Soil Isolation

| Objective | Recommended medium |
|---|---|
| General broad survey | DRBC, MEA (2-3 g/L), GAN |
| Basidiomycetes (selective) | LGB agar |
| Slow-growing/rare species | Low-nutrient SNA + filter paper; dilute MEA |
| Osmotolerant/xerophilic | DG18, MY5-12 agar |
| Alkalophilic fungi | Horikoshi I or II; ACMA |
| Thermophilic fungi | Emerson YS agar (+ rose bengal + gentamicin) |
| Chytridiomycetes | CFD water; 0% Czapek's agar; bait methods |
| Soil basidiomycetes (enrichment) | Leonians agar (ML); PDA |

## Soil Chytridiomycetes

**Collection:** Collect both fresh and air-dried soils; chytrids survive drying. Top 5 cm of soil.

**Isolation:** Use bait methods (see taxon-specific/aquatic-fungi.md — Chytridiomycetes section for detailed protocols). Primary baits: pollen, cellophane, hemp seed, chitin, keratinous substrata.

**Note:** Chytrid species diversity increases with sampling effort; different soil types share some but not all species; seasonal variation within sites is typically low between years.

## Endophytic Fungi

**Isolation principle:** Endophytes are internal to healthy plant tissue; surface sterilization is critical to exclude epiphytes and environmental contaminants.

**Standard surface sterilization protocol:**
1. Clean plant surface with water; blot dry
2. Immerse in 70–95% ethanol for 1 minute
3. Transfer immediately to 0.5–10% sodium hypochlorite for 5–10 minutes (time varies by tissue)
4. Rinse 2–3× in sterile distilled water
5. Dry on sterile filter paper; cut tissue into segments
6. Place cut segments face-down on isolation medium; press gently to ensure contact
7. Incubate and monitor for hyphal outgrowth from tissue margins

**Surface-sterilization controls:** Include uninoculated plates to verify no residual bleach; include surface-wipe plates (swab tissue surface before sterilization onto an agar plate) to document starting epiphyte load.

**Media:** MEA (2-20 g/L), PDA (dilute), CMA, Starch-Milk agar, AWA + antibiotics. Many protocols use 2% MEA with chloramphenicol (100 mg/L) and streptomycin (100 mg/L).

**Tissue types:** Bark, leaves, roots, wood, seeds — each has specific sterilization times and protocols (see Ch. 12 for detailed taxon/tissue-specific guidance).

## Wood and Lignicolous Fungi (General)

**Collection:** Fresh decorticated wood, logs, stumps; various substrata. Use sterile tools; label thoroughly with field data.

**Moist chamber incubation:** Dampen substrata slightly (don't soak); incubate in sealed plastic bags/boxes at room temperature or habitat temperature in alternating light/dark; examine every 1–2 weeks for fruiting bodies for 3–6 months.

**Isolation:** Cut mature fruiting bodies; aseptically excise small piece of fertile tissue (avoid outer surface); transfer to agar. For ascomycetes: single-spore isolation (see Freshwater Ascomycetes protocol in aquatic-fungi.md — same technique applies to terrestrial ascomycetes).

**Media:** PDA, MEA (10-20 g/L), Hypomyces Fruiting Agar (HFA), Polypore Extract-Infusion Agar.

## Coprophilous Fungi

**Collection:** Fresh dung samples from herbivores (cow, horse, rabbit, deer) or carnivores. Collect in sterile containers.

**Moist chamber incubation:**
1. Place dung in shallow plastic or glass containers with moistened filter paper
2. Cover; incubate at room temperature or 25°C in alternating light/dark
3. Examine daily with dissecting microscope for 4–8 weeks
4. Fungi appear in succession: Zygomycetes first (days 1–7), Ascomycetes second (1–4 weeks), Basidiomycetes last (2–8+ weeks)

**Media:** Dung-extract agar (DEA), oat-dung extract agar (ODA), acidic PDA (add 6 drops 50% lactic acid when pouring), MEA, CMA. See media-recipes.md.

## Insects and Arthropods

**Collection:** Collect living or recently dead insects; keep cool during transport. For host-specific fungi: collect host insects from known habitats.

**Incubation:** Place insects/arthropods in small chambers with high humidity; observe for sporulation over 1–4 weeks at appropriate temperature.

**Isolation of entomopathogens:** Pick sporulating material under dissecting microscope; streak on appropriate media. For Beauveria, Metarhizium: PDA, Sabouraud dextrose agar, SDAY. For Entomophthorales: specialized protocols needed (see liquid nitrogen preservation — these often do not survive standard preservation).

## Key General Principles

1. **Colony density on plates matters:** Target 5–30 colonies per 100-mm plate (fewer is better). High density causes competitive exclusion of slow-growing taxa.

2. **Incubation temperature:** Always consider the source habitat temperature. Incubating at room temperature when the source habitat is cold water will dramatically change the species recovered.

3. **Incubation duration:** Most surveys check plates for 1–4 weeks. Some rare or slow-growing species only appear after extended incubation (up to 3 months). For marine fungi: some species only appeared after 2 years in plastic bags (Kohlmeyer & Volkmann-Kohlmeyer 2004).

4. **Media selection is a major source of bias:** No single medium is appropriate for all fungi. Use multiple media in parallel when comprehensive inventory is the goal.

5. **Light/dark cycling:** Many sexual reproductive structures require alternating light and dark cycles (12h/12h). Standard lab conditions (constant light or dark) may prevent sporulation.

6. **Contamination is always an issue:** Use antibiotics judiciously; be aware of taxon-specific antibiotic toxicities (see media-recipes.md — Antibiotics section for details).

7. **Document everything:** Record collection data, isolation methods, media used, incubation conditions, and all observations — not just positive results. See documentation-vouchers.md.
