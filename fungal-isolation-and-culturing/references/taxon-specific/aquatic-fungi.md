# Aquatic Fungi — Collection, Isolation, and Culturing

> Source: Foster, Bills & Mueller (2004) *Biodiversity of Fungi: Inventory and Monitoring Methods*. Academic Press.  
> Primary chapters: Ch. 23 (Freshwater Habitats — Shearer et al.), Ch. 24 (Marine & Estuarine Mycelial Eumycota & Oomycota — Kohlmeyer, Volkmann-Kohlmeyer & Newell), Ch. 26 (Fungi Associated with Aquatic Animals — various).

## Overview of Freshwater Fungal Groups

| Group | Key features | Preferred habitats |
|---|---|---|
| Chytridiomycetes | Zoosporic; monocentric or polycentric thalli | All freshwater; mud, sediment, water column |
| Peronosporomycetes (Oomycetes) | Straminipilous; mycelial; zoospores | Well-aerated freshwater, moist soils; some marine |
| Freshwater Ascomycetes | ~450 spp.; perithecia/apothecia on submerged substrata | Submerged leaves, wood, macrophytes |
| Aquatic hyphomycetes (Ingoldian) | Tetraradiate/branched conidia; ~300 spp. | Lotic habitats; submerged deciduous leaves/wood |
| Aeroaquatic hyphomycetes | Air-trapping conidia; sporulate in air | Lentic habitats with fluctuating water levels |

## I. CHYTRIDIOMYCETES (Freshwater)

### Collection Techniques

**Bait collections (standard):** Place sterile bait materials in dishes of water with collected substrate (soil, detritus, water). Incubate 2–14 days at 10–20°C. Different baits attract different species — use multiple bait types and methods simultaneously.

**Standard bait materials:**
- Pollen (pine, cattail/Typha, others) — floated on water surface
- Cellophane squares (~1 cm²) — floated on surface
- Hemp seed halves (autoclaved) — placed in water
- Chitin (shrimp shells, arthropod exoskeleton)
- Keratinous substrata (shed snake skin, hair)
- Onion epidermis, cellulose strips

**Preparation of bait dishes:**
1. Collect substrate (soil/sediment/detritus) in plastic bags with field notes (date, location, GPS, habitat, water temp, air temp, pH, other parameters)
2. Keep cool; culture within a few hours if possible (samples may remain viable at ambient temperature up to 1 month — Seymour & Fuller 1987)
3. Make thick slurry by adding sterilized distilled, CFD (charcoal-filtered distilled), or deionized water
4. Place enough slurry to coat one-third of bottom of deep Petri plates (100 mm × 20 mm)
5. Add water to one-third to one-half full
6. Float bait on surface or place in mixture
7. Incubate at 10–20°C; examine at 2–3 days; additional incubation up to 2 weeks recommended
8. **Note:** incubating at temperatures approximating natural habitat temperatures has yielded "rare" Peronosporomycetes — applicable principle for chytrids too

**In situ baiting:**
- Place bait materials in mesh bags, aluminum tea balls, or dialysis tubing
- Suspend in aquatic habitat or bury in sediment
- Examine after several days to weeks
- Essential for isolating Rhipidiaceae (for that group: use solanaceous or rosaceous fruits in plastic-coated wire mesh cages in stagnant/slow-moving shallow water; examine at 10 days for pustulelike outgrowths)

**CFD water (charcoal-filtered distilled water):** Promotes formation of motile spores in many aquatic zoosporic fungi. Critical for some chytrid work. Prepare by filtering distilled water through activated charcoal.

### Culturing

**Pure culture technique:**
1. Pick up zoospores from bait cultures with a fine pipette or mounted hair
2. Transfer to agar slants or plates with CFD water or 0% Czapek's agar (no sucrose)
3. For monocentric chytrids: use 125-mm screw-cap culture tubes with agar slants; water from condensation when cooling can be tipped over colonies to spread zoospores
4. Polycentric species do not require tipping
5. Incubate at optimum growth temperature until colonies established; then refrigerate (5°C) for storage
6. Most temperate isolates remain viable at 5°C for at least 3 months
7. Keep duplicate tubes in separate incubators as backup

**Long-term storage:** Many chytrid cultures retain viability after freezing and storage in liquid nitrogen (Barr & Babcock 1994; Boyle et al. 2003). See preservation-storage.md.

**Key references:** Emerson (1958), D.J.S. Barr (1987), Whisler (1987).

### Biodiversity Assessment

- Quantitative methods not well-refined for this group
- Bacteriological (MPN) and limnological methods usable when substrate is particulate
- For bait-only recovery: rank crude abundance by number of bait bags with target species / total bait bags
- Thorough studies of limited areas with reliable species IDs more valuable than ambitious but dubious surveys
- Bait in all seasons across multiple years; sample all available microhabitats
- Different collection methods do not always reveal the same species — use all approaches

## II. PERONOSPOROMYCETES / OOMYCETES (Freshwater)

> **Taxonomic note:** This group (formerly Oomycetes) is now placed in Kingdom Straminipila (Chromista), not Fungi. However, methods for collection and isolation are identical to true fungi. Includes *Saprolegnia*, *Achlya*, *Aphanomyces*, *Pythium*, *Phytophthora*, Rhipidiaceae.

### Collection

**Indirect methods (standard):**
1. Collect water or moist/dry soils/sediments in small plastic bags
2. Keep cool (approximate habitat temperature); culture within a few hours (or store at ambient temp for up to 1 month)
3. In lab: make thick slurry with sterilized distilled, CFD, or deionized water
4. Add hemp seed halves (autoclaved) and/or 1 cm² shed snake skin (soaked 1 minute in boiled water, then floated on surface)
5. Examine plates after 2–3 days; continue up to 2 weeks
6. Incubate at habitat temperature for best yield of "rare" species

**Dilution plating (Pythiaceae):**
1. 50 g fresh weight soil → 250 ml 0.08% water agar (sequential dilutions to achieve 1:100 to 1:500)
2. Spread 1-ml aliquots of desired dilutions on fresh (≤36 hours old) VP3 agar plates
3. Incubate at 20–22°C for 42–48 hours
4. Rinse gently with tap water; examine
5. Examine again after another 24 hours; transfer resulting hyphae to fresh agar plates

**In situ baiting (Rhipidiaceae):**
- Solanaceous or rosaceous fruits in plastic-coated wire-mesh cages
- Suspend in stagnant or slow-moving shallow water
- Examine at 10 days for pustulelike outgrowths containing Peronosporomycetes
- Rinse vigorously; remove and tease pustules apart for microscopic examination
- Also works in CO₂-high/O₂-low environments (shady bogs, swamps) for Leptomitales

**Centrifugation to concentrate propagules (Fuller & Poyton 1964):** Recommended to increase yield from individual samples.

### Isolation and Culturing

**Single hypha isolation:**
1. Remove single hyphal filaments from actively growing colonies in gross cultures using flame-sterilized forceps
2. Transfer to Petri plates with agar-based nutrient media: cornmeal, PYG, PDA, V8 juice agar
3. Incubate until colonies form

**Single-spore isolation:**
1. Streak water containing motile/encysted spores with flame-sterilized bacteriological loop along surface of water agar plates
2. Incubate at 20–22°C for 12–24 hours
3. Locate germinating spores; cut from plate; "plant" on fresh agar-based nutrient medium
4. Incubate 3–5 days until recognizable colonies form

**Bacterial contamination removal:**
- **Antibiotic regimen:** 400 units penicillin G + 0.2 mg streptomycin-SO₄ per ml of medium (added to molten media just after autoclaving)
- **Caution:** Some antibiotics also inhibit peronosporomycetes — verify no toxicity for target taxon
- **Pimaricin:** Controls contaminating eumycetes; **do not use for Halophytophthora spinosa** (toxic)
- **Streptomycin:** Can be toxic to oomycetes — use with caution
- **Chloramphenicol:** Toxic to oomycetes at high concentrations; use only at low concentrations
- **Germanium dioxide:** Inhibits diatoms
- **Alternative — agar-flip method:** Invert plate; run sterile probe between agar and plate to detach agar; remove piece from newly uncovered lower agar surface (bacteria confined to surface layer); plant on fresh medium
- **Heat ring method (Raper 1937; Seymour & Fuller 1987):** 12 mm × 12 mm glass ring, dipped in 90% ethanol, flamed, placed on agar surface — residual heat embeds ring; hyphae placed inside grow under ring and emerge outside bacteria-free

**Maintenance:**
- Maintain on nutrient agar plates or slants, or on appropriate baits in water culture
- Water cultures: cut blocks of hyphal-colonized agar → place in Petri plates with sterilized CFD/distilled/deionized water; OR apply baits to edge of colony on agar, remove after 24 hours, place in Petri plates with fresh sterile water and additional bait
- Cultures generally maintained 1–6 months at 20–22°C without transfer
- **Mite control:** Glycerin moat — invert empty Petri plate cover; pour thin layer of glycerin into cover; replace bottom half; space between lips fills with glycerin to trap mites; place moat below stack of culture plates

**Long-term preservation:**
- Dehydration: possible but only with some species
- Oil overlay: possible but inconsistent
- Liquid nitrogen freezing: successful for some species only
- **Most reliable method:** Maintain on appropriate nutrient agar slants or water cultures on species-appropriate bait

**Key references:** Johnson (1956), Sparrow (1960), Seymour (1970), Stevens (1974), Fuller & Jaworski (1987), Dick (1990a).

## III. FRESHWATER ASCOMYCETES

### Collection

**Direct collection:**
1. Collect living and dead macrophytes from edges of ponds, lakes, bogs, swamps, wetlands, drainage ditches, temporary water bodies, submerged/partially submerged woody debris, submerged deciduous tree leaves
2. Use hand, sturdy rake, tree trimmer (for macrophytes rooted in deep water), saw/twig cutters/ax (for large woody debris)
3. Place in plastic bags with paper toweling to absorb excess water
4. Store on ice in cooler until transport; include detailed field notes: date, GPS, elevation, substrate identity, substrate state (living/dead/decomposing/submerged), habitat type, air and water temperature, pH, and other water chemistry

**Moist chamber incubation (critical step):**
1. Rinse substrata with sterile tap, distilled, or deionized water
2. Remove aufwuchs (algae, protozoa, bryozoans, etc.) and mud/sand by hand or gentle scraping
3. Transfer to moist chambers: glass or plastic Petri plates with moistened filter paper; plastic refrigerator storage boxes; or plastic bags with moistened paper toweling
4. Material should just cover bottom — avoid dense layering (obscures ascomata, depletes O₂)
5. Pour off standing water before incubation
6. Incubate at habitat temperature in alternating light/dark (12h/12h) — this enhances sexual reproduction
7. Examine before incubation for species present; then examine periodically for ~6 months

**Bait substrata:**
- Attach bait twigs or wood blocks (from identified wood sources) to masonry bricks/cement blocks; submerge in habitat for specified periods
- Assemble in packs with nylon twine; tether to pier or tree
- Dead macrophyte leaves/stems in nylon mesh bags; submerge in habitat
- Plan for both flood and drought conditions when selecting/placing baits

**Why NOT to use plating (indirect collection):**
- Ascospores of many freshwater ascomycetes germinate poorly or not at all on plates
- Those that germinate are overgrown by fast-growing weedy fungi (Mucor, Penicillium, Trichoderma)
- Confirming identities requires inducing reproduction — often long periods required
- Some species never reproduce in culture → identification impossible without molecular methods
- Very labor-intensive and often futile

### Identification

Freshwater ascomycetes are identified by morphology and anatomy of ascomata and morphologies of hamathecium, asci, and ascospores.

**Key microscopic techniques:**
- Squash mounts for most known species — usually sufficient
- Thin sections for unknown/problematic specimens (razor blade rough sections; freezing microtome for fresh specimens, especially Discomycetes)
- Embed in low-viscosity resin; ultra-microtome for fresh or rehydrated dried specimens
- Squash mounts of centrum in water + dilute India ink suspension → reveals gelatinous coatings on physes, asci, ascospore sheaths, and appendages
- Remove ink by pulling distilled water under coverslip to reveal appendages stained by ink
- Stain ascus tips with Melzer's reagent and aqueous cotton blue to determine apical apparatus
- Photograph fresh material immediately — drying/preserving alters appearance (appendages become invisible)

### Isolation and Culturing (Single-Spore)

**From closed ascomata:** Crush mature ascomata in 2–4 ml sterile distilled water → spore suspension.

**Accumulation at ostioles:** Remove with flamed, cooled needle; stir into sterile water. Agitate vigorously with tube agitator to separate ascospores and dislodge bacteria.

**Spore suspension plating on AWA:**
1. Pour suspension onto AWA (antibiotic water agar) plates
2. Allow to stand 1–2 hours for spores to settle
3. Decant excess water gently
4. Incubate at slight angle to drain remaining water

**AWA plate streaking (alternative):** Crush ascomata on AWA surface with fine needle; spread ascospores and asci on agar surface.

**For discomycetes (apothecia):**
1. Attach small piece of filter paper with water/petroleum jelly to lid of AWA plate, ~1 cm from edge
2. Place apothecium on filter paper, surface facing down toward agar
3. Put lid on bottom plate; rotate ~45° every 30 minutes so ascospores are discharged in circular path around plate

**For pyrenomycetes (forcible ascospore discharge):**
1. Mount small piece of substratum with perithecia directed downward on filter paper attached to lid
2. Invert plate so agar receives upwardly discharged ascospores

**Critical timing:** Examine plates microscopically with transmitted light after 24 hours of incubation; mark position of germinated ascospores. Transfer germinated ascospores within next 24–48 hours (plates left longer may be overgrown by contaminants). If ascospores do not germinate within 3 days, still transfer to nutrient medium — this often stimulates germination.

**Transfer to nutrient media:** CMA, MEA, PYG, or YPSS. Make at least 4–8 isolates per species (heterothallic species need multiple strains for reproduction).

**Long-term storage (6–12 months):** Add strips of moistened balsa or birch wood to culture tubes before autoclaving.

**Extended storage (years):** Colonized agar pieces in small sterile vials with 16% glycerin solution at –40°C.

**Inducing ascomata for identity verification:**
1. Grow cultures on alfalfa stems, wood strips, or natural substrata (Juncus, Typha, willow roots)
2. 4–6 cm pieces in 50 ml sterile distilled (or filtered habitat) water in 125-ml Erlenmeyer flasks; autoclave 1 hour
3. Inoculate with mycelium from stock tubes; rotary shaker at 100 rpm at 22°C until well-colonized; OR stationary at ambient temp, swirled 1–2× per day
4. Transfer to Petri dish moist chambers; seal with Parafilm; incubate 18–22°C in alternating light/dark (12h/12h)
5. Daylight fluorescent light; additional near-UV light may stimulate fruiting in some species
6. Fruiting time varies greatly: 2 weeks to 6 months

**Testing for aquatic hyphomycete anamorphs:** Check cultures for aquatic hyphomycete states (these have been reported for several freshwater ascomycetes); also examine for pycnidial anamorphs on standard laboratory media.

## IV. AQUATIC HYPHOMYCETES (Ingoldian/Mitosporic Freshwater Fungi)

### Group Characteristics
- Produce elongate or branched conidia — tetraradiate, sigmoid, or otherwise adapted for dispersal in flowing water
- ~300 species described (as of 2004)
- Most colonize decaying deciduous leaves/wood of riparian origin in lotic habitats
- Aeroaquatic forms (conidia trap air for flotation) more common in lentic habitats with fluctuating water levels; sporulate in air, not water

### Collection from Substrata

1. Collect submerged decaying leaves, twigs, subsamples of logs; place in plastic bags with field data; ice during transport
2. Wash leaves gently in tap, distilled, or stream water
3. Cut 10–20 leaf disks with handheld paper hole punch → place in deep Petri dishes (100 mm × 20 mm) in ~30 ml sterile tap/distilled/stream water
4. Include petiole material (often abundant conidia producer)
5. **Single leaf or 10–20 disks only per dish** — too much material inhibits sporulation and promotes bacteria/protozoa
6. Pour off standing water before incubation
7. Incubate at collection stream temperature (aquatic hyphomycetes are temperature-sensitive); alternating light/dark helpful
8. Examine after 24–72 hours with dissecting microscope, elevated stage, subsurface illumination (conidia are opaque; show up against dark background)

**From woody debris:** Place twigs in cylindrical tubes with water; pass stream of air bubbles along twig → excellent conidial production → membrane filter the conidial suspension → stain in lactic acid with cotton blue.

### Collection from Foam (Stream Foam)

Foam naturally concentrates conidia of aquatic hyphomycetes and is a rich source for diversity surveys.

1. Scoop foam from natural foam traps using small wide-mouth glass/plastic jars (baby food jars ideal)
2. **Critical:** Do not take up water with sample; decant standing water from jar to avoid diluting conidia
3. **Minimum 4 samples per site** for statistical analysis; label immediately with collection number and habitat data
4. Keep on ice for transport
5. Store up to 1 month at 13°C, or fix with FAA (Formalin-acetic acid-alcohol)
6. Examine: drop of foam on slide with coverslip; OR spread on slide, dry, cover with lactic acid + cotton blue + coverslip
7. Remove sand grains and debris before coverslipping to maximize visibility

**Limitation:** Slow-flowing streams and neutral/basic pH streams produce little foam. See Iqbal (1993) for artificial foam method.

### Membrane Filtration of Water (Quantitative)

*(Iqbal & Webster 1973b; now widely used)*

1. Pass 500–1,000 ml stream water through cellulose membrane filter (47 mm diameter, 5–8 µm pore size) using handheld vacuum pump
2. **Minimum 4 water samples filtered per site** for statistical analysis
3. Air-dry membrane filters in plastic Petri dishes; label top and bottom; store (dried samples remain useful for up to 3 years)
4. **To examine:** Cut 1 × 1 cm square from filtration area with razor blade; place on glass slide
5. Add drop of lactic acid with cotton blue/trypan blue (0.1% w/v) gently to one side to avoid dislodging conidia; allow full hydration before coverslip
6. Warm at 60°C for 1 hour to clear filter (not usually necessary)
7. Identify and quantify conidia at 200× under compound microscope

**Major limitation:** Cannot identify species solely from spore morphology — many ascomycetes have sigmoid spores that converge with aquatic hyphomycete conidia; type of conidiogenesis must be known.

### Isolation of Aquatic Hyphomycetes

1. Locate conidia in dishes of submerged substrata or foam under dissecting microscope with below-illumination
2. Pick up individual conidia with pipette drawn out to form capillary tube, or mounted needle/hair
3. Place on low-nutrient medium with antibiotics (e.g., 0.1% MEA; Webster & Descals 1981; Descals 1997) or AWA
4. Move conidia along agar surface to dislodge attached bacteria; mark final location on dish underside
5. **Alternative:** Shake leaf disks/fragments in sterile distilled-water blanks; plate 1–2 ml of conidial suspension onto AWA; allow conidia to settle for several hours; decant excess water; incubate at 45° angle at ambient temperature
6. **Critical timing:** Identify germinated conidia and transfer to nutrient medium within 24–36 hours
7. **Standard culture media:** MEA (Webster & Descals 1981; Descals 1997); also CMA and YPSS work well or better for many species (Shearer, unpublished)

### Deposition/Vouchers (Aquatic Hyphomycetes)

- Conidia are delicate and hyaline — do not preserve well on dried substrata
- Traditionally: slides fixed in lactophenol with acid fuchsin or cotton blue, sealed with nail polish
- Many investigators switching to lactic acid as preservative (phenol is carcinogenic)
- **Strongly recommended:** Double coverslip technique (Volkmann-Kohlmeyer & Kohlmeyer 1996) for permanent slides
- Foam samples fixed with FAA can be deposited in herbaria (FAA must be replaced periodically)
- Deposit photographs of conidia and conidiogenesis with microscope slides

## V. MARINE AND ESTUARINE FUNGI (Eumycota)

### Overview and Ecology

- ~444 species of higher marine fungi described (as of 2004); primarily Ascomycotina
- Not a taxonomic group — defined ecologically:
  - **Obligate marine fungi:** grow and sporulate exclusively in marine/estuarine habitat; permanently or intermittently submerged
  - **Facultative marine fungi:** normally freshwater or terrestrial; able to grow (and possibly sporulate) in marine environment
  - **Halotolerant terrestrial fungi:** restricted to immersed parts of estuarine/marine plants
- Deepest record: 5,315 m depth
- **Critical limiting factor:** Dissolved oxygen — low O₂ in water/sediments inhibits/prevents fungal growth (no growth at <0.30 ml/L; growth at 1.26 ml/L)
- Saprotrophs decompose cellulose (driftwood, pilings, mangrove roots, marsh plants) and grow on washed-up algae, seagrass leaves, animal products (chitin, keratin, tunicin, calcium carbonate)

**Greatest species diversity:** Intertidal habitats — sandy beaches, jetties, salt marshes, mangal (mangrove habitat)

**Substrate types and specialized habitats:**
- Arenicolous fungi — sandy beaches
- Caulicolous/foliicolous fungi — senescent marsh plant leaves and culms (*Juncus roemerianus*, *Spartina* spp.)
- Manglicolous fungi — submerged parts of mangrove trees (bark, decorticated wood, dead/damaged roots, pneumatophores)
- Lignicolous fungi — submerged branches and trunks of dead trees
- Corallicolous fungi — dead corals (24 spp. from CaCO₃ substrata)
- Endolithic fungi — inside corals, calcified algae, calcareous substrata

### Collection — Eumycota

**Seasonal timing:**
- Tropics: sample year-round (small temperature fluctuations)
- Temperate: highest diversity in summer months
- Subtropical: sample at least twice — late winter and summer (temperate and tropical species overlap)

**Arenicolous fungi (sandy beaches):**
- Collect foam with spoon-type skimmer; pour into bottle; refrigerate a few hours until air bubbles disappear; fungal propagules sink → pick up with pipette for microscopy
- Alternative: collect wet flotsam (algae, seagrasses, wood, feathers) at water's edge; mix with moist (not wet) sand; add foam if available; half-fill sterile plastic bag (e.g., Whirl-Pak 23 cm × 11 cm)
- Store at room temperature for months; examine regularly under dissecting microscope for fruiting bodies
- Some slow-growing ascomycetes first appeared after 2 years of incubation

**Caulicolous/foliicolous fungi (marsh plants):**
1. Cut senescent leaves/culms of *Juncus roemerianus*, *Spartina* spp. at base close to rhizome
2. Store in plastic bags refrigerated or frozen until examined, or air-dry
3. Split culms/leaves lengthwise with razor blade; scan under dissecting microscope for embedded fruiting bodies
4. Marine species at base; facultative marine in middle; halotolerant terrestrial at tip

**Manglicolous fungi (mangroves):**
- Cut dead/damaged roots (proproots of *Rhizophora*, pneumatophores of *Avicennia*) close to sediment
- Examine under dissecting microscope
- Look for submerged branches and trunks of dead trees; remove wood pieces containing fruiting bodies
- Walk at low tide or snorkel at high tide in mangrove habitats

**Corallicolous fungi:**
- Lift coral rocks looking for ascomata on underside (very tedious — may need to lift dozens of rocks)
- Examine lower side of dead coral slabs
- Look for *Koralionastes* spp. associated with crustose sponges on lower sides of dead coral slabs

### Isolation — Eumycota (Marine)

1. Air-dry substratum to facilitate isolation
2. Excise top of ascoma or conidioma with sterile razor blade
3. Remove contents of centrum with fine needle
4. Transfer to drop of sterile distilled water in inoculation loop
5. Streak loop on seawater agar with antibiotics (see media-recipes.md: Seawater Agar — SA)
6. **For hyphomycetes (harder to isolate):** Carefully pick off sporulating hyphae with fine sterile needle under dissecting microscope → transfer to drop of sterile water → process as above for ascospores
7. Cut small blocks of agar bearing germinating spores/conidia → transfer to plates/tubes of same medium without antibiotics
8. Store at ~20°C for temperate species; ~25°C for tropical/subtropical species

**Key reference:** Hyde & Pointing (2000) *Marine Mycology: A Practical Approach*.

### Maintenance

- Maintain cultures in plastic dishes (60 mm diam. × 15 mm high) with tightly fitting lids at room temperature (~22°C)
- OR in sterile dilute seawater in sealed containers at 15°C
- Cultures can be maintained 6–9 months before transfer
- Reduce water loss with nonpolar sealing film (Parafilm M) around plates
- **Warning:** Marine mycelial oomycotes can die suddenly in culture for no apparent reason → maintain multiple replicates of valuable cultures

**Rescue of apparently dead cultures:** Remove half the agar aseptically; fill depression with sterile dilute seawater + autoclaved peanut bit; submerge cut-out agar in separate dish of sterile dilute seawater with peanut bit.

## VI. MARINE AND ESTUARINE OOMYCOTES (Halophytophthora and related genera)

### Key Taxa

| Genus | Notes |
|---|---|
| *Halophytophthora* | 14 spp. (as of 2004); most common decomposers of submerged fallen leaves in mangrove systems; *H. vesicula* most commonly encountered |
| *Pythium* | Some marine species; *P. grandisporangium* from intertidal grass |
| *Haliphthoros* | Parasitizes ova/larvae of marine crustacea and mollusks |
| *Salilagenidium* (formerly *Lagenidium*) | Parasitizes ova/larvae of marine crustacea |

**Salinity note:** *Halophytophthora* uses dilute seawater (15 g salts/L) — NOT full-strength seawater — for most procedures. Autoclaving seawater may eliminate its stimulatory effect on some species (Fell & Master 1975).

### Collection — Marine Oomycotes

**Leaf-disk sampling (primary method):**
1. Collect decaying submerged leaves at low tide (walk) or high tide (snorkel) — mangroves, saltmarshes, temperate coastal sites
2. Rinse leaves in field to remove surface clay/silt
3. Transport in plastic bags with just enough water to prevent drying; protect from heat and direct sunlight
4. **Process as soon as possible after return to lab** (ideally under laminar-flow cleanhood)
5. Drain leaves; optionally surface-treat with biocide (for internally active mycelia only)
6. Remove standard-size leaf disks from decaying leaves with cork borers (sterilize borers between samples to prevent cross-contamination)
7. Plate disks on oomycote-selective medium: **A/T Oomycote Medium** (see media-recipes.md) — particularly effective for *Halophytophthora* spp.
8. **Critical medium note:** Do not use high concentrations of chloramphenicol (toxic to oomycotes; up to tenfold reduction in *H. vesicula* frequency); do not use streptomycin or pimaricin (*Halophytophthora spinosa* is killed by pimaricin)
9. Incubate at approximate field temperatures for 3–4 days in **dark** (to minimize loss of amphotericin's antieumycetic activity); then alternate light/dark (12h/12h) to stimulate zoosporangia formation
10. Read plates 10–20 days after sampling (depending on temperature); do not delay reading — sporangia may deteriorate through autolysis or be destroyed by predators
11. Inspect disk surfaces, edges, disk/agar interface, surrounding mycelia, and plate edges; use subsurface transmitted and surface illumination; dissecting microscope at 25–100×

**For quantitative data:** Place yellow (senescent) leaves from terrestrial trees into seawater sites → determine frequencies of occurrence for zoospores/zoospore cysts of specific oomycotes after various periods.

**Seasonal sampling:** Collect during both cold and warm seasons — some species are temperature-limited (e.g., *H. exoprolifera* to colder; *H. spinosa* to warmer intervals).

**Water column sampling:** Baiting with autoclaved plant material; water can be sampled but vacuum filtration renders zoospores nonviable; continuous centrifugation has potential but undertested for marine systems.

### Inducing Zoospore Release (for Identification)

Zoospore release behavior and morphology of release structures differ among species and **must be induced for each type of sporangium to confirm species identifications**.

General methods:
- Changes in salinity and temperature
- Exposure to light
- Brief drying
- Exposure to sterols or phospholipids

Species-specific notes:
- ***H. vesicula*:** Produces sporangia and releases zoospores after agar containing mycelium is submerged in dilute seawater (15 g/L)
- ***H. exoprolifera*:** Both temperature and available plant lipids are important
- ***H. bahamensis* and *H. epistomium*:** Isolated sporangia placed on slide in dilute seawater (15 g/L or less); zoospores released naturally or by artificial stimulation

**Slide maintenance for observation:** Support slides above bottoms of closed Petri dishes containing 1 ml distilled water (damp chamber); observe periodically. Dilution by adding freshwater on one side and drawing out with absorbent paper on other side may elicit zoospore release (but avoid too rapid dilution — causes sporangia to burst). Microcontinuous-flow chambers are ideal (Quesnel 1969; Amon et al. 1987).

### Isolation — Marine Oomycotes (Pure Culture)

**Method 1: Hyphal-tip culturing**
1. Cut hyphal tips from mycelial edges in area of plate appearing to contain only one species (confirm by presence of sporangia/oospores)
2. Transfer aseptically under laminar-flow cleanhood to new A/T Oomycote Medium plates
3. As mycelia grow away from contaminants carried with tips, cut and transfer tips again to medium without inhibitors
4. Place ~1 mm³ autoclaved plant material (peanut, sesame, or safflower seed) on agar to provide lipids needed for sporangium/oospore genesis (Fell & Master 1975; Ho et al. 1992)
5. Use low agar concentration (e.g., 0.5%) to stimulate oospore production
6. Incubate in alternating light/dark (12h/12h) until sporangia developed for identification
7. For cultural purity: place single sporangium in sterile seawater; bait zoospores onto autoclaved plant bits; transfer to new agar plates

**Method 2: Sampling and culturing sporangia**
1. Cut single sporangia or small groups from original leaf-sampling plates
2. Confirm single-species identity with compound microscope
3. Place on A/T medium; push across agar surface with fine sterile nichrome needle (produce spatulate point with small hammer, or fine point with file)
4. Mark final destinations on bottom of plate
5. Hyphae produced from direct sporangial germination (*H. exoprolifera*) or from mature unreleased zoospores (*H. vesicula*)
6. For species not producing hyphae this way (*H. bahamensis*, *H. epistomium*): place sporangia on slide in dilute seawater until zoospores released; immediately place sporangia on agar medium and manipulate as above; or transfer drop of seawater with released zoospores to A/T medium

**Method 3: Baiting zoospores**
1. Isolate and confirm identity of sporangia at 400× magnification
2. Submerge separated sporangia in dilute sterile seawater (15 g/L or less)
3. Bait released zoospores onto autoclaved plant bits on plates or in well slides
4. Transfer plant bits to new A/T medium plates with inhibitors as soon as zoospore release observed
5. Isolated zoospores will encyst; may germinate to form small mycelia; excise and transfer hyphal tips

**Transferring cultures:** More successful if a piece of original plant material from the plate is included in the transfer.

### Isolation from Infected Fish/Amphibians (*Saprolegnia*, *Aphanomyces*)

**From lesions:**
1. Select fish with fresh lesions (avoid necrotic/secondary-infected tissue)
2. Surface-disinfect skin around lesion with 70% ethanol; dry
3. Aseptically scrape or biopsy mycelial growth from lesion margin
4. Transfer to plates containing: dilute PDA, cornmeal agar, or V8 juice agar with antibiotics (penicillin G 200–400 units/ml + streptomycin 0.1–0.2 mg/ml)
5. Alternatively: place infected tissue on hemp seed in Petri plate with sterilized distilled water; incubate; pick hyphae from growing edge

**Baiting from water:** Place hemp seed halves or other bait materials in water from affected tank/habitat; examine for hyphal growth after 24–72 hours.

**Isolation media for *Saprolegnia*:**
- Dilute PDA (1/10 strength) with antibiotics
- Glucose peptone yeast agar (GPYA)
- Cornmeal agar with antibiotics
- CFD water or deionized water with antibiotics (for zoospore production)

**Pure culture:** Hyphal tip transfer from leading edge of colony on agar plates; or pick germinating zoospores from water agar.

**Confirmation of identity:** Observe oogonia, antheridia, and oospores (sexual structures); zoosporangium discharge patterns; morphology of primary and secondary zoospores (diplanetism).

### Baiting for Aquatic Animal-Associated Oomycetes

**Hemp seed baiting:**
1. Autoclave hemp seed halves at 121°C for 15 min
2. Place 2–3 half-seeds in Petri plates with water from habitat
3. Add sample material (infected tissue fragments, sediment, water)
4. Incubate at habitat temperature; examine daily for 1–2 weeks

**Snake skin baiting (especially for *Saprolegnia*, *Achlya*):**
1. Heat shed snake skin to boiling for 1 minute; soak in water
2. Float on surface of bait plates
3. Incubate at habitat temperature

## Key Practical Notes

1. **CFD water** is not just "distilled water" — activated charcoal filtration is required to make true CFD water; substituting deionized water is acceptable but CFD water is the traditional standard shown to promote zoospore formation.

2. **Salinity for marine oomycotes:** Use dilute seawater (15 g salts/L) for most *Halophytophthora* work, not full-strength seawater. For open-ocean marine fungi, use 37‰; for inshore/estuarine sampling, use ~15‰ brackish.

3. **Oxygen is critical:** Dissolved oxygen limits marine fungal distribution. Low-O₂ environments (stagnant water, deep sediments, "minimum oxygen zones") will dramatically reduce yield.

4. **Temperature matters:** Incubate at habitat temperature whenever possible — many "rare" species are temperature-limited and appear only at specific temperatures.

5. **Mite control in Peronosporomycete cultures:** Glycerin moat is preferred first-line control; dieldrin is last resort (see media-recipes.md for formula — extremely toxic).

6. **Cultural purity confirmation:** For marine oomycotes, always verify purity using a compound microscope (not just dissecting scope) to confirm single-species identification before depositing cultures or publishing records.

7. **Vouchers are non-negotiable:** "Lists of records that cannot be verified are mere waste paper" (Dennis 1968). All published records must be backed by deposited vouchers in recognized repositories.
