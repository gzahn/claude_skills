# Lichenized Fungi — Inventory and Culturing

> Source: Foster, Bills & Mueller (2004) Ch. 9 (Lichenized Fungi — Will-Wolf, Hawksworth, McCune, Rosentreter & Sipman).

---

## Overview

Lichens are composite organisms: a fungal partner (mycobiont, predominantly Ascomycota) in stable symbiosis with a photosynthetic partner (photobiont — alga or cyanobacterium). ~13,500 described species (~20% of all described fungi). Unlike most other fungi, lichen thalli have definable boundaries, layered structure, and species-specific morphology, making them more amenable to quantitative ecological sampling than most fungal groups.

**Key ecological features:**
- Found in every terrestrial habitat capable of supporting photosynthesis; slow-growing, long-lived, stress-tolerant
- Primary limiting factors: substratum chemistry/stability/longevity; light (competition with faster-growing plants); moisture availability
- Important winter food for reindeer, caribou, deer; emergency food for other large mammals
- Sensitive biological indicators of air quality (especially SO₂ pollution)

**Taxonomic note:** Scientific names refer to the fungal partner (mycobiont). The photobiont has an independent name. It is not formally possible to "name a lichen" — only the lichen-forming fungus.

---

## Morphological Categories

- **Leprose:** mealy particles of intertwined fungal hyphae and algal cells
- **Crustose:** embedded on/in substrate surface; multiple layers; medullary fungal hyphae attached directly to substratum
- **Squamulose:** small flakes of thallus
- **Foliose:** leaf-like; distinct upper and lower surfaces; attached by rhizines
- **Fruticose:** shrubby or string-like; stalked, tufted, or pendulous; uniform outer layer

## Identification

**Chemical spot tests** (most important for sterile material):
- **K:** 10% KOH in water
- **C:** undiluted commercial bleach
- **P or PD:** saturated alcohol (95% ethanol) solution of p-phenylenediamine, or Steiner's Stable Solution
- **I:** Lugol's Iodine (sometimes Melzer's Iodine)
- **N:** 10–50% nitric acid solution
- **LCB:** lactophenol-cotton blue (staining compound)

Apply to thallus surface, internal tissues (exposed with razor/scalpel), or compound microscope sections of fruiting bodies or thallus tissue.

**Thin-layer chromatography (TLC):** Standard method for secondary metabolite identification. Required for many species where color differences from spot tests are subtle. Computer programs available for Perugia lichens (Mietzsch et al. 1993).

**Photobiont identification:** Requires isolation into unialgal cultures for species-level identification.

## Field Collection

**Equipment:**
- Hand lens; rock hammer and chisel (hard mineral substrata); stout knife or wood chisel (woody/soft substrata); hand pruning shears; paper packets or bags; small card boxes (fragile soil-surface specimens)
- Vertical cliff faces or tree canopies: special equipment and training required

**Field protocols:**
- **Never** use watertight or water-resistant containers — molds can overgrow specimens within a few hours under warm humid conditions
- Store one specimen per paper bag/packet; record substratum, location, and date in pencil or waterproof ink
- Record abundance data linked to individual field specimens at time of collection
- Wet/damp specimens must be **air-dried thoroughly within a few hours** of collection; never store damp
- **Conservation:** collect minimum specimens needed for accurate identification; if only one specimen is seen, collect only half of it (field etiquette)
- Epiphytic lichens from recent litterfall can be collected without impact (they will die anyway)

**Sampling protocols:**
- Minimum: one specimen per species encountered in whole study area as voucher
- Species distinguishable in field by expert: record presence/abundance directly; collect 2–4 specimens from widely separated sites for confirmation
- Crustose lichens: nearly always require collection for lab identification
- In poorly surveyed regions: one specimen per species per site

## Culturing (Mycobiont Only)

Living cultures of lichen-forming fungi (without photobionts) are **difficult to maintain** due to:
- Extremely slow growth
- High susceptibility to contamination

**What works:**
- ~1/3 of lichenized fungal species successfully cultured on first attempt (Crittenden et al. 1995)
- Shaken liquid cultures preferred over solid media (to overcome slow growth)
- Sophisticated temperature-controlled freezing/thawing protocols for liquid nitrogen preservation

**Where to obtain cultures:**
- American Type Culture Collection (ATCC) has the largest publicly available collection
- Few species available from main microbial culture collections

**Long-term storage:** Liquid nitrogen with individualized freezing/thawing protocols (Smith & Onions 1994). See preservation-storage.md.

**Isolated mycobionts** form colonies similar to non-lichenized fungi on agar — no typical lichen structures. Colonies grow very slowly and are compact.

## Voucher Specimens

- Archive dry in acid-free paper packets; stable for centuries if kept in dry atmosphere
- Soil specimens: dip substratum (not lichen) in water-soluble glue to reduce fragmentation; glue to stiff card after drying
- For historical buildings/monuments: make microscope preparations directly from specimens in field to facilitate identification without collecting
- Electronic and hard-copy archives of raw data should be stored in standard formats (ASCII minimum) at managing institution

## Diversity Patterns (Context for Survey Design)

- **World's richest lichen areas:** southern temperate rain forests > northern temperate forests > high-latitude zones (tropical richness may be underestimated due to inadequate collections)
- **Substrate patterns:** Rocky substrata support much of diversity in temperate/cold zones; foliicolous lichens almost exclusively tropical
- **Useful benchmarks for survey planning:** up to 227 species/km² on bark and wood alone in deciduous temperate woodland (UK); >400 spp. epiphytic lichens in <200 ha old-growth tropical lowland forest

**General sampling guidance:**
- **10–20% of regional species pool** typically found in a single biodiversity survey of 50,000 ha
- Field:lab time ratio: approximately 1:4 to 1:10
- Use both specialist lichenologists and trained non-specialist field technicians; specialists for identification confirmation of all vouchers
- Visit all substrate types: bark, rock, soil, dead organic matter, leaves (tropics)

## Taxonomic Orders Containing Lichenized Species (Ascomycota)

14 orders include lichen-forming representatives; 5 are exclusively lichenized. Main orders:
- **Lecanorales** (7,150 spp.) — majority of lichen-forming fungi
- **Ostropales** (1,600 spp.)
- **Arthoniales** (1,000 spp.)
- **Pyrenulales** (500 spp.)
- **Peltigerales** (510 spp.)
- **Teloschistales** (570 spp.)
- **Verrucariales** (700 spp.)
- **Lichinales** (280 spp.; all with cyanobacteria)

A few Basidiomycota form lichens: some *Omphalina* (Agaricales), *Multiclavula* (Cantharellales), *Dictyonema* (Stereales).
