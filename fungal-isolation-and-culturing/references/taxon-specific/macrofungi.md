# Terrestrial and Lignicolous Macrofungi — Culturing

> Source: Foster, Bills & Mueller (2004) Ch. 8 (Terrestrial & Lignicolous Macrofungi — Lodge et al.).

---

## Overview

Culturing macrofungi is useful for distinguishing similar taxa, identifying species (especially in Xylariaceae, Hypocreaceae, other pyrenomycetes where the anamorph is required for accurate ID), and for biodiversity studies. Saprobic fungi are generally easier to culture than ectomycorrhizal species. 

**General media:** PDA, MEA (10–20 g/L), Leonians agar (ML; especially good for basidiomycetes), Hypomyces Fruiting Agar (HFA), V8-juice agar. See media-recipes.md.

## Method 1: Culturing from Basidiospores / Ascospores (Primary Method)

### Spore Drop Directly onto Agar

1. Attach ascomata or hymenial surface of basidiome to the **lid** of a Petri plate of agar (using petroleum jelly, small chips of agar, or water drops)
2. Tilt wrapped plate at steep angle; align hymenium of agarics (gills vertical in the direction of tilt) or pores of polypores
3. This produces a cascade with high spore density at top, decreasing toward bottom — allows both polyspore and single-spore isolates from same plate
4. **Remove the source specimen as soon as spores are detected** — additional incubation only leads to contamination by Fusarium, Trichoderma, etc.
5. Rotate the lid periodically (30 seconds to 10 minutes) to obtain distantly spaced spores for single-spore isolates

**Timing guidelines:**
- Tropical fungi: remove hymenium within **12–24 hours**; spores usually drop within first 2 hours
- Temperate/boreal fungi: may require several days to a week for spore drop
- Dark spores: 8–12 hours usually sufficient to detect spore print
- Examine by inverting plate and focusing through agar with dissecting microscope

**Critical cautions for tropical basidiomycetes:**
- Barometric pressure changes (>several hundred meters elevation change) can prevent spore drop — set up spore drops in the field if lab is at very different elevation
- **Do NOT refrigerate tropical basidiomycetes before culturing** — >1 hour in refrigerator causes failure to drop spores; cultures held at 5–10°C often die
- This cold-temperature effect has NOT been observed in temperate basidiomycetes

### Spore Prints from Field-Collected Material

- Collect spore prints in the field onto sterile 60-mm Petri plates (agarics, most aphyllophorales, some ascomycetes)
- Alternatively: collect on paper or aluminum foil; air-dry; use later (works for some temperate fungi)
  - Morel spore prints on aluminum foil: viable when frozen for a year or more
  - **Armillaria:** air-dried spores >several days old generally not viable
  - **Marasmius:** air-dried spores viable for only ~2 hours
  - Many tropical rain forest fungi: spores sensitive to desiccation; viable for only 1–2 hours
  - **When viability is in doubt: drop spores directly onto agar surface**
- To obtain spore prints from air-dried basidiomycetes: refrigerate basidiome 2–3 days in agar-filled Petri dish; return to room temperature to develop new basidia and produce spore print (works mainly for certain temperate aphyllophorales)
- Resupinate basidiomycetes: more likely to produce spores if hymenium is taken from wood, not bark

### Suspension Plating / Streaking

Transfer drop of spores suspended in sterile water to agar plate; streak over agar surface with piece of agar or flame-sterilized loop or rod.

### Single-Spore Isolation

1. Germinate spore deposit on agar plate
2. Identify germinated spores well-separated from other spores using dissecting microscope with adjustable mirror (spores appear bright against dark background)
3. Remove with fine sterilized needle
4. Transfer only germinated spores clearly isolated from others to individual culture plates
5. Rotate lid periodically to spread spores; scan and mark germinated ones at 8–12 hours

### From Ascomata (Xylariaceae and Pyrenomycetes)

- Use mature ascomata (moist and turgid; air-dried and rehydrated are also acceptable — soak in water 30 min to 2 h)
- For forcible discharge: **invert Petri dish**, place ascomata on lid below agar surface, allow spores to shoot upward (avoids contamination by non-forcible-discharge fungi)
- Elevate perithecia if necessary (small clay piece, small lid) so discharged spores reach agar
- If ostioles are clogged: pick off spore deposits with sterile needle; streak onto agar with drop of water from lid
- If ascomata fail to discharge: extract ascal contents with sterile scalpel, flamed needle, dental tool, or insect pin; streak onto agar
- **Do NOT culture ascomal wall tissue or internal stromatal cells** of small ascomycetes — you risk isolating a contaminant rather than the intended fungus (Hypoxylon has been isolated from internal stromatal tissue of Xylaria)

## Method 2: Tissue Culture from Basidiomes

For basidiomycetes only (not recommended for ascomycetes).

1. Split basidiome down the middle with sterile scalpel
2. Remove small piece of tissue from **upper stipe or pileus** (sterile internal tissue) with sterile scalpel or forceps
3. **Tearing** the basidiome apart instead of cutting reduces contamination risk and tissue damage
4. Place extracted tissue directly on small test tube slants
5. For tough reexed or ungulate species: cut partway through basidiome; tear the portion to be sampled

**Rhizomorphs and mycelial strands:**
1. Fasten vegetative structures (hyphal strands, rhizomorphs, cordons) to the **lid** of an agar plate
2. Clean hyphae grow from broken ends and contact agar within 1–2 days
3. Transfer hyphal tips to fresh agar plates
4. For species difficult to culture from hyphal tips but growing from larger masses: invert agar plate with rhizomorph to reduce contamination risk after contact is made; transfer large masses on agar chips (some placed on new agar with hyphal side up)

**Hyphal bridges (for thin resupinate basidiomycetes):**
1. Place specimen on its substratum inside a moist chamber
2. Aerial hyphae or mycelial strands that attempt to bridge air gaps toward paper toweling or lid can be transferred to agar plates

## Method 3: Direct Isolation from Wood/Substratum

For fungi that produce fruiting bodies infrequently:

1. Expose clean wood surface with flame-sterilized knife; OR thoroughly clean substratum surface with 70% ethanol or 10% sodium hypochlorite wash
2. Use flame-sterilized increment borer to obtain core; place in sterile container (plastic drinking straws, or aluminum foil for friable substrata)
3. Before plating: dip chips or cores in 95% ethanol and flame for surface sterilization; OR wash under strong stream of distilled water
4. For large pieces of wood or roots: submerge in 0.5% sodium hypochlorite for 10–15 minutes; then cut with flamed knife
5. Plate chips on agar; isolate fungi as they grow from core or chip

**Drill shaving method:** Drill into log; sample resulting shavings at given depths. Collect directly into culture tubes or plates; allow to grow. Skips the intermediate steps of the coring method.

## Practical Notes for Tropical vs. Temperate Macrofungi

| Parameter | Tropical | Temperate |
|---|---|---|
| Spore drop timing | Within 2 hours; >12 hours = contamination | Days to a week may be needed |
| Refrigeration before culturing | **NEVER** — kills cultures | Usually fine |
| Spore viability | 1–2 hours in many species | Hours to days; some storable frozen |
| Spore print on aluminum foil | Not reliable | Works for many species (e.g., morels viable frozen 1+ year) |
| Immature specimens | Place in moist chamber with original substratum; return to forest | Can usually hold in lab |

## Culture Identification of Basidiomycetes

For nonsporulating basidiomycete isolates from wood, soil, or other substrata, use cultural characters systematically:

- **Macroscopic:** colony color, texture, margin, exudates, soluble pigments, growth rate
- **Microscopic:** hyphal system type (monomitic/dimitic/trimitic), clamp connections, cystidia, crystal deposits
- **Nuclear staining:** DAPI or acriflavin + fluorescence microscopy (see stains section in media-recipes.md)
- **Physiological:** tolerance to benomyl, cycloheximide, NaCl; DBB staining (see soil-fungi.md)
- **Spot tests:** laccase (syringaldazine), tyrosinase (p-cresol), peroxidase (syringaldazine + H₂O₂)
