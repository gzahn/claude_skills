# Soil Fungi — Isolation and Culturing

> Source: Foster, Bills & Mueller (2004) Ch. 13 (Saprobic Soil Fungi — Bills et al.) and Ch. 14 (Fungi in Stressful Environments — Zak & Wildman).

---

## Soil Collection

- Collect from A1 horizon (upper mineral soil) for most surveys; also sample litter/humus layers (they harbor distinct mycotas)
- Remove litter and humus from ~20 cm² area; sterilize digging tool in 70% ethanol and dry before each sample
- Collect 6–10 plugs (10–30 g) per site in labeled sterile bags; composite or analyze separately
- In arid/semi-arid zones: also sample subsurface moist horizon (distinct mycota from drier surface soil)
- Record: site, date, vegetation, climate, soil type, horizon, land use history
- Process promptly; store at 4°C if delayed

**Scale guidance:** Minimum of 600–1000 isolates per site for comprehensive inventory; 180–250 isolates per site for quantitative comparative data on principal species. Maximum workload for two experienced researchers: ~2000–3000 isolates at a time.

## Principal Isolation Methods

### Suspension Plating (Quantitative Standard)

1. Disperse soil in sterile water, 0.05–0.2% agar, or 0.1–0.2% carboxymethylcellulose (retards sedimentation)
2. Run trial dilutions to find soil:water ratio yielding **5–30 colonies per 100-mm plate** (low density critical)
3. Pipette aliquots onto agar surface OR mix with cooled agar and pour
4. Incubate at 17–24°C or in situ temperature; then room temperature
5. Isolate all colonies in sequence by hyphal tip transfer until predetermined number reached

**Media:** GAN, DRBC, CMC, dilute MEA (2 g/L) + antibiotics. Include colony-restricting agents: rose bengal (suppresses colony expansion; incubate in dark), cyclosporin A (2–10 mg/L), dichloran. Use nutrient-poor media to avoid bias toward fast-growing weedy fungi.

### Warcup Soil Plates

1. Disperse minute quantities (0.2–5.0 mg) of finely pulverized soil across bottom of sterile Petri dish
2. Pour cooled (still liquid) agar over soil; agitate gently to disperse particles
3. Incubate as above

**Alternative (Drechsler's method):** Sprinkle minute quantities of fresh soil or humus directly onto water agar or very dilute cornmeal agar — creates microcosms; good for parasitic fungi, oomycetes from root fragments.

**Advantage:** Simpler than suspension plating; yields basidiomycetes not recovered by suspension plating.

**Rhizosphere variant:** Remove tiny root fragments aseptically; place in vials with 5 ml sterile water or 0.1% sodium pyrophosphate; agitate 1 minute; plate washed fragments or 0.1–1.0 ml of wash water on agar.

### Particle Filtration ("Soil Washing")

Wash soil through nested sieves to produce size-separated particles; plate particles on isolation media. Favors fungi from mycelial fragments over spore-forming taxa.

- Recommended particle size: 50–80 µm (Bååth 1988); 105–210 µm for forest litter (Bills & Polishook 1994)
- Plate constant volume of washed particles on isolation media; count and identify colonies
- For microhabitat data: separate organic vs. mineral fractions before plating
- After particle filtration, plate some particles on **LGB agar** (lignin-guaiacol-benomyl) specifically for saprobic basidiomycetes

**Note:** Gives better results for species with chlamydospores in organic particles; requires practice.

## Fractionation and Enrichment Techniques

### Selective for Ascosporic Fungi: Ethanol Pasteurization

1. Soak soil (15 g) in 70% ethanol for 10 minutes; wash with sterile water before plating
2. Dramatically increases recovery of: Eurotiales, Chaetomium, Xylaria, coprophilous ascomycetes, ascosporic yeasts
3. Reduces Zygomycetes and mitosporic fungi with thin-walled conidia
4. Ethanol alone performs the same as ethanol + mild heat; use ethanol only

### Selective Basidiomycete Isolation (LGB Agar)

Use particle filtration (to remove spores of mitosporic fungi and Zygomycetes) followed by plating on **Lignin-Guaiacol-Benomyl (LGB) agar** (see media-recipes.md). Guaiacol acts as colorimetric indicator of lignin-modifying enzymes — colonies causing brown color change are candidate basidiomycetes. Scan at 40–100× to select those, avoid Alternaria, Trichoderma.

Recovered 67 basidiomycete isolates from 64 soil samples from 35 plots (Thorn et al. 1996).

**Additional tip:** Warcup or Drechsler soil plates on very fresh soils may yield basidiomycetes that appear as white cottony colonies among soil crumbs but fail to colonize the medium — pick hyphae with needle/forceps under dissecting scope; transfer to nutrient media.

### Thermophile Enrichment (for rare thermophilic species)

Add recalcitrant carbon sources (cellulose, carnauba wax, chitin, keratin, soluble starch, lactose + gentamicin, or lignin) to agar; incubate 12–25 days at 50°C; keep moist (add sterile water periodically). Then prepare pour-plates from each incubated bottle; incubate at 50°C.

### Density-Gradient Centrifugation

- **Percoll gradient:** Separates different-sized spores before plating; increases taxa recovered vs. suspension plating alone
- **Sucrose gradient:** Preferentially concentrates large ascospores (Paden 1967)
- Useful for: basidiospores, sclerotia in soil; separating nematophagous fungi (large-spored predators from small-spored endoparasites)

## Media for Specific Soil Fungal Groups

| Target group | Medium | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| General broad survey | DRBC, GAN, dilute MEA | Incubate in dark (rose bengal) |
| Soil basidiomycetes | LGB agar | After particle filtration |
| Thermotolerant/thermophilic | Emerson YS agar + gentamicin + rose bengal | Incubate at 45–50°C |
| Psychrophilic | MYP agar (Malt-Yeast-Peptone) | Incubate at 0–4°C for up to 10 weeks |
| Osmophilic/halophilic | MY5-12, MY10-12, halophile agar | Adjust aw with glycerol, NaCl, or sucrose |
| Xerophilic | DG18 (dichloran-glycerol-18) | Final aw 0.955 |
| Alkalophilic | Horikoshi I or II, ACMA | pH 8.5–11; autoclave after pH adjustment |
| Acidophilic | Standard media acidified with tartaric/lactic/citric acid | Adjust pH after autoclaving |
| Halophilic | Czapek glucose-substituted + 15–20% NaCl | Instant Ocean or sea salts also used |
| Rock-inhabiting | DRBC or dilute Czapek-Dox (1:50) | Wash samples first to remove spores |
| Oligotrophic | Silica-gel medium | Requires trace-contamination-free glassware; muffled at 500°C |
| Chytridiomycetes | Bait methods; CFD water | See aquatic-fungi.md for protocols |

## Incubation Conditions by Target Group

| Group | Temperature | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Mesophilic (general) | 17–24°C or in situ | Incubate 4–14 days; then room temp |
| Thermophilic | 45–50°C primary; 40°C subculture | Never incubate primary cultures below 40°C; use sealed containers to prevent drying |
| Psychrophilic | 0–4°C | Incubate 3–10 weeks; examine twice weekly |
| Halophilic | 28°C | 6–8 weeks in dark |
| Xerophilic | Room temperature | Extended incubation |
| Phoenicoid (post-fire) | Room temperature | Sterile straw/filter paper added to soil to induce fruiting; 1–50 days |

**Key principle:** The incubation temperature determines what component of the community is recovered. Incubating soil from cold habitats at room temperature completely misses psychrophilic species (Carreiro & Koske 1992 found entirely different communities at 0°C vs. 25°C from the same soil).

## Identifying/Sorting Isolates

**Preliminary sorting on agar slants:**
- Transfer all colonies to large glass test tube slants (15–20 mm diam. × 125–150 mm); inoculate at a single point at the same level in each tube
- Incubate at constant temperature under variable light (periodic fluorescent light or indirect daylight) to promote colony zonation
- After 1–2 weeks: sort into morphological types by colony characteristics (color, texture, margin, exudates, soluble pigments)
- Transfer representatives of all morphological types to Petri dishes for identification

**Inducing sporulation in sterile isolates:**
- Transfer to multiple media: alpha-cellulose agar, CMA (± autoclaved banana leaf), oatmeal agar, V8-juice agar, SNA
- Prolonged incubation (weeks to months) sometimes needed
- If persistently sterile: characterize by physiological tests (see below), or use rDNA sequencing

**Physiological spot tests for nonsporulating isolates:**

| Test | Basidiomycete | Ascomycete |
|---|---|---|
| Benomyl (10 mg/L) | Tolerant | Sensitive |
| Cycloheximide (2 mg/L) | Sensitive | Tolerant |
| NaCl (10 g/L) | Sensitive | Tolerant |
| DBB stain (after 1M KOH pretreatment) | Positive | Negative |

Staining with DAPI or acriflavin + fluorescence microscopy: uninucleate = haploid monokaryon; binucleate = dikaryotic basidiomycete; multinucleate = ascomycete. Staining large-diameter thin-walled hyphae with trypan blue or ammoniacal Congo red may reveal septal apparatus (simple pores in ascomycetes; dolipore septa in holobasidiomycetes).

**Substrate utilization tests** (useful for characterization): cellulose (Cellulose Azure Broth Tubes), lipids (Tween-20 agar), creatine (Creatine agar), milk solids (BCP-MS-YE), urea (Christensen's urea broth). Colorimetric spot tests for laccase (syringaldazine), tyrosinase (p-cresol), peroxidase (syringaldazine + H₂O₂).

## Key Field Notes on Soil Fungal Communities

**Predictable community associations (useful for planning surveys):**
- Arctic/alpine/boreal: Cylindrocarpon, Chrysosporium, Mortierella, Oidiodendron, Penicillium spp.
- Cold bogs/coniferous swamps: yeasts, Tolypocladium, Trichoderma spp., sterile dematiaceous forms
- Beech forests (Fagus): striking similarities NA ↔ Europe in principal species
- Desert/subtropical/tropical: Aspergillus dominant; melanized thick-walled conidia common
- Agricultural soils: Acremonium, Arthrinium, Cladosporium, Drechslera, Fusarium, Microdochium, Phoma
- Humid tropical forests: Aspergillus, Penicillium, Trichoderma; many minor/rare genera in litter

**Biodiversity sampling guidance:**
- Species-isolate curve asymptote: 60–150 species per 600–1000 isolates (temperate/tropical soils)
- Common taxa of a homogeneous vegetation type: determinable from ~250 strains from 6–10 samples or a composite
- Different soil types share some species but harbor distinct assemblages; seasonal variation within a site is typically low between years
- Chytrid diversity increases with sampling effort; at least 14 samples per site; sample in each major season
