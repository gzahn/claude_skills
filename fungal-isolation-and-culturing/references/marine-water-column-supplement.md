# Marine Fungal Isolation — Supplemental Methods Reference
## Water Column, Sediment, Substrate Sampling, In Situ Cultivation, and Advanced Methods

> Supplemental reference file for the fungal-isolation-and-culturing skill.
> Synthesized from 35 papers (2002–2025) to extend Ch. 24 coverage of the
> Foster, Bills & Mueller (2004) source textbook.
> Coverage: water column filtration, marine media recipes, macroalgae/sponge/

## I. WATER COLUMN SAMPLING — Filtration Methods

### Pore Size Selection

| Target group | Filter pore size | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Yeasts | 0.45 µm nitrocellulose | Textbook (Foster et al. 2004) |
| General marine fungi (water column) | 0.22 µm polyethersulfone (1 L vacuum unit) | Mitchison-Field & Gladfelter 2021 |
| Filamentous fungi (hyphae/spores) | 1–8 µm polycarbonate | General practice |
| In situ microbial traps | 0.4–0.6 µm polycarbonate | Overy et al. 2019 |
| FiChip fungal adaptation | 0.22 µm nylon | Li et al. 2023 |
| Plankton tow concentration | 504 µm mesh net + 0.22 µm vacuum | Mitchison-Field & Gladfelter 2021 |

### Gladfelter Lab Protocol — Three-Treatment Water Column (Mitchison-Field & Gladfelter 2021)

**Materials:**
- 0.22 µm PES filter unit, 1 L (VWR cat. no. 28199-792)
- Vacuum pump, sterile cotton swabs, sterile spreaders, Bunsen burner
- 1–2 L sterile jugs; plankton net 504 µm mesh (optional)

**Procedure:**
1. Collect 1–2 L ocean water into sterile jugs. Record GPS, temperature, pH.
   Optional: use plankton net and funnel for surface tows (match tow speed/duration
   across samples for density estimates).
2. Mix jug. Pipette **300 µL unconcentrated** onto plates → Treatment 1.
3. Fill filter reservoir with 1 L water; apply vacuum until ~10 mL remains.
4. Pipette **300 µL concentrate** onto plates → Treatment 2.
5. Continue filtering to thin layer; swab filter with sterile cotton swab and
   streak plate, OR cut filter and place face-down on agar → Treatment 3 ("filter").
6. Spread all treatments with sterile spreader (flame between plates). Let dry.

**Plating design:** 2 replicates per treatment × 3 media types = 18 plates/sample minimum.

**Incubation:** 18–20°C. Monitor daily week 1; some species (e.g., black yeasts)
take >1.5–2 weeks to appear. Continue monitoring for several weeks.

## II. MARINE MEDIA RECIPES

### Seawater-Based Standard Media (Mitchison-Field & Gladfelter 2021)

All prepared with **filter-sterilized seawater** (collected or artificial: 36 g/L
Instant Ocean) to 1 L. Autoclave 40 min liquid cycle, 10 min cooling.

**MEA + seawater:**
- Malt extract 20 g · Peptone 6 g · Dextrose 20 g · Agar 12 g · Seawater to 1 L

**PDA + seawater:**
- Potato dextrose broth (Sigma P6685) 24 g · Agar 12 g · Seawater to 1 L

**YPD + seawater:**
- Yeast extract 10 g · Peptone 10 g · Dextrose 20 g · Agar 12 g · Seawater to 1 L

**Antibiotic cocktail (add after cooling ~20 min post-autoclave):**
- Carbenicillin 100 µg/mL · Chloramphenicol 25 µg/mL · Tetracycline 10 µg/mL
- Prepare 250× stock in filter-sterilized water (4 mL stock per 1 L media).
- Vortex before addition (precipitate forms). Store aliquots at −20°C months.

### Arctic/Cold-Water Media (Hagestad et al. 2020)

For high-latitude seawater, sediment, driftwood, macroalgae collections:
- **ASMEA:** 0.4% malt extract agar in artificial seawater
- **ASCMA:** cornmeal agar in artificial seawater
- **1.0ASAsco:** 1% dried homogenized *Ascophyllum nodosum* in artificial seawater
- Antibiotics: streptomycin 30 µg/mL + tetracycline 30 µg/mL
- Incubation: 4–10°C

### Endophyte Isolation Media (Kjer et al. 2010 — Marine Natural Products Protocol)

**Medium A** (isolation from sponges, algae, mangroves):
- Bacto agar 15 g · Malt extract 15 g · Artificial sea salt 24.4 g (10 g for mangroves)
- Chloramphenicol 0.2 g · pH 7.4–7.8 · dem. water to 1 L
- Autoclave 121°C 20 min. Pour ~25 mL/plate. Use within 1 week.

**Liquid Wickerham's medium** (fermentation/liquid culture):
- Yeast extract 3 g · Malt extract 3 g · Peptone 5 g · Glucose 10 g
- Artificial sea salt 24.4 g · pH 7.2–7.4 · dem. water to 1 L
- 300 mL per 1 L Erlenmeyer; autoclave 121°C 20 min

### Alga-Specific Host-Extract Media (Gnavi et al. 2017; Garzoli et al. 2018)

For isolating fungi specifically associated with a host alga:
- **CMASW:** 17 g CMA dissolved in 1 L filtered seawater
- **FASW (Flabellia):** 1 g fw alga in 100 mL seawater, boil 30 min at 60°C,
  filter; add 18 g agar; seawater to 1 L
- **PASW (Padina):** same approach with *P. pavonica* biomass
- Antibiotics: gentamicin 80 mg/L + piperacillin/tazobactam 100 mg/L
- Incubate: 15°C for 15 d (psychrotolerant), then 24°C for 45 d (mesophilic)

The two-temperature incubation captures both cold-adapted marine fungi and the
more typical mesophilic generalists; skipping the 15°C stage misses cold-adapted taxa.

### Lignicolous/Sea Foam Media (Overy et al. 2019)

- Minimal medium: 0.1% glucose + 0.01% yeast extract + 1.8% agar in aged seawater
- Cornmeal seawater agar, V8 seawater agar (suppresses yeast overgrowth)
- Antibiotics: penicillin G 300 mg/L + streptomycin sulfate 500 mg/L
  (add to cooled agar before pouring; chloramphenicol can be autoclaved in)

## III. SUBSTRATE-SPECIFIC PROTOCOLS

### Near-Shore Coastal Sediment (Mitchison-Field & Gladfelter 2021)
1. Fill 50 mL tubes 1/2–2/3 with sediment, include surrounding water.
2. Invert to mix. Pipette 300 µL slurry onto plates; spread immediately.

### Sediment from Research Vessels — Grab Sampling (Hagestad et al. 2020)
1. Van Veen grab or equivalent. Remove surface with sterile knife.
2. Scoop interior sediment into immediately-closed Petri dish.
3. Streak inoculation loop across sediment; streak onto ≥3 media types.
4. Incubate at habitat temperature.

### Deep-Sea Sediment — Culture-Dependent Approaches (Singh et al. 2012)
- Central Indian Basin sediment cores at ~5000 m depth, ~40 cm below seafloor.
- Media panel: PDA, MEA, SDA, CMA, YPD, Rose Bengal agar, Czapek Dox agar.
- Temperature gradient: 4°C and 28°C (aerobic); anaerobic conditions also tested.
- Fungal genera recovered: *Aspergillus*, *Penicillium*, *Cladosporium*, *Hortaea*,
  *Cerrena* — many with high ITS divergence from GenBank (86–97% similarity).
- Culture-independent recovered 46 OTUs; many novel lineages detectable only by
  molecular methods, not culture.

### Deep-Sea Fungi — Hadal / Pressure Cultivation (Chen et al. 2021)
- Pressurized vessels: 100 MPa hydrostatic (≈ Challenger Deep pressure, ~11,000 m).
- Incubation at 4°C (hadal temperature) and 28°C, aerobic and anaerobic.
- 19 pressure-tolerant fungal strains recovered: *Acremonium*, *Aspergillus*,
  *Aureobasidium*, *Cladosporium*, *Exophiala*, *Gymnoascus*, *Penicillium*, etc.
- Most represent species newly specialized to the hadal environment.

### Deep-Sea Cold Seep — Early-Diverging Lineages (Nagahama et al. 2011)
- Culture-independent PCR of methane cold-seep sediment (JAMSTEC, Pacific).
- 35 phylotypes identified; 12 were early-diverging basal fungi (Chytridiomycota +
  LKM11 clade). Basal fungi dominated total fungal clones.
- LKM11 clade = environmental-only sequences from aquatic habitats; may include
  *Rozella*-allied lineages (earliest-branching Fungi).
- Implications: deep-sea marine sediments harbor ancient fungal lineages unlikely
  to be recovered by standard plating onto rich media.

### Macroalgae (Hagestad et al. 2020)
1. Collect into sterile bags from intertidal sites.
2. Cut to ~1 × 1 cm pieces.
3. Surface sterilize: dip 30 s in 70% ethanol; rinse in autoclaved MilliQ.
4. Place 2–3 pieces per plate onto ASMEA, ASCMA, and algal-extract media.
5. Incubate; transfer mycelium once visible onto fresh antibiotic-free plates.

### Macroalgae — Full Homogenization (Gnavi et al. 2017; Garzoli et al. 2018)
For Mediterranean green (*Flabellia petiolata*) and brown (*Padina pavonica*) algae:
1. Sonicate thalli (30 s × 3), wash 3× in artificial sterile seawater.
2. Homogenize in 20 mL sterile filtered seawater using Ultra-Turrax sterile device.
3. Dilute homogenate 1:10 in sterile seawater; plate 1 mL per 120 mm plate.
4. Use CMASW + host-extract medium (FASW or PASW).
5. Incubate: 15°C 15 d → 24°C 45 d. Record CFU/g dry weight.
- Gnavi et al. found 64 fungal taxa on *F. petiolata*; Garzoli et al. found 134 taxa
  on *P. pavonica*, including 29 new marine records.

### Driftwood (Hagestad et al. 2020; Overy et al. 2019)
1. Remove surface with sterile knife; cut 0.5 × 1 cm interior pieces.
2. Plate 2–3 pieces/plate; monitor for months.
3. Fruiting bodies: moisten with seawater; let dry until spore discharge; transfer spores.

### Coral / Sponge Holobiont (Mitchison-Field & Gladfelter 2021)
1. Collect ~10 g fragment; rinse with sterile seawater.
2. Add 1 mL filtered seawater per 1 g fragment mass.
3. Grind with mortar and pestle to homogeneous slurry.
4. Plate both: 100 µL supernatant (clearer layer) and 150 µL slurry (thick layer).
   Cut 1 cm from pipette tip to avoid clogging for slurry.
5. Spread; incubate at habitat temperature.

## IV. SEA FOAM COLLECTION (Overy et al. 2014, 2019)

Sea foam traps appendaged conidia and ascospores of arenicolous ascomycetes.
1. Collect fresh foam into sterile centrifuge tubes through ~2 mm mesh sieve.
   Dried/collapsed foam: scrape and resuspend in sterile seawater.
2. Add double-strength antibiotic solution before plating.
3. Settle at 4°C several hours; concentrate by centrifugation.
4. Plate by dilution-to-extinction in 48-well plates.
5. **Do not store >24 h** — marine yeasts continue growing even at 4°C.

## V. IN SITU CULTIVATION — iChip and Related Devices

### Background and Performance

| Method | Recovery increase vs. standard plating | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Original diffusion chamber | 30,000% | Kaeberlein et al. 2002 |
| iChip (soil/marine bacteria) | 5–300-fold | Berdy et al. 2017 |
| FiChip (fungi, mangrove sediment) | ~2× species; 45 novel strains/700 isolates | Li et al. 2023 |
| HTC extinction culturing (bacteria) | 14–1,400-fold | Connon & Giovannoni 2002 |

### Original iChip Design — Nichols et al. 2010

- Machined Delrin (polyoxymethylene) plate, 72 × 19 × 1 mm, 384 through-holes (1 mm dia.)
  arranged in two 192-hole arrays; top/bottom plates provide rigidity.
- Membranes: **0.03 µm pore polycarbonate** (Whatman/Osmonics 25 or 47 mm).
  Blocks cell migration; allows diffusion of nutrients and growth factors.
- Assembly: central plate dipped into dilute cell suspension in liquid agar.
  Agar solidifies, trapping ~1 cell/hole. Membranes applied both sides; outer plates
  tightened by screws (pressure seal — no adhesive needed).
- Returned to environment in situ; diffusion of naturally occurring growth factors
  supports growth of previously unculturable taxa.

### Berdy et al. 2017 — Simplified Protocol

Full step-by-step protocol in Nature Protocols (DOI: 10.1038/nprot.2017.074).
Key departures from Nichols original:
- Device simplified for commercial use (fewer machined components).
- Soil incubation 1–4 weeks; retrieval and processing 1–4 h.
- Domestication step: colonies grown in iChip are passaged to conventional media;
  some fail (obligate in situ growers), but many establish.

### Modified iChip — Gauthier et al. 2025 (JoVE; Recommended Starting Point)

96-well plate adaptation; easiest to build in-lab without machining.

**Construction:**
1. Remove bottoms of 4 × 96-well plates (VWR 10062-900) using 4–5 mm cork borer.
2. Cut **0.05 µm polycarbonate membranes** (Whatman Nuclepore WHA113502; 7.6 × 11 cm).
3. Adhere membrane to plate bottom with **100% silicone aquarium sealant**
   (Marineland cat. no. 31003). Sealant must seal edges without blocking well openings.
   Dry ≥24 h.

**Media (SMS — Succinate Minimal Salts):**
- Potato starch 0.1 g · Casamino acids 1 g · Casein digest 0.125 g
- Bacteriological agar 15 g · Water to 1 L
- Autoclave 20 min at 121°C.

**Cell suspension preparation:**
1. 1 g soil/sediment + 10 mL sterile water; vortex 10 min; settle 10 min.
2. Take 0.5 mL supernatant → Tube A; make 10-fold serial dilutions → A through H
   (8 tubes = 10⁻¹ to 10⁻⁸ equivalents).

**Inoculation:**
1. Sterilize assembled plates: 95% ethanol ≥15 min; remove; UV 15 min in laminar flow.
2. Rinse plates 3× sterile water; remove 95% ethanol residue.
3. Pipette 360 µL sterile SMS into first column → controls (no cells).
4. Mix 45 mL SMS cooled to 50°C + tube E cell suspension; immediately pipette
   360 µL/well with multichannel pipette. Work fast — agar solidifies quickly.
   Do not reheat agar-cell mixture (kills cells).
5. Seal top with PCR plate cover.

**Incubation:**
1. Place iChips in covered box on ~1 inch of original substrate (soil/sediment),
   membrane-side down. Cells receive diffusing growth factors.
2. Incubate 25°C in dark.
3. Check at 1 week: contamination = overgrowth in all wells of a plate.
4. At 6 weeks: use plates with growth in **<25% of wells** for colony isolation
   (ensures single colonies per well).

**Colony isolation:**
1. Cut plate cover around individual well with sterile blade.
2. Pierce colony with sterile streaking tool; streak onto full-size SMS plates
   (four-quadrant method). Incubate 25°C.
3. Colonies that don't survive: return plate to soil and re-incubate (continued
   domestication). Expected success rate ~50% of iChip colonies to conventional plates.

**Success criteria:**
- At least one plate with <25% wells containing growth; no growth in controls.
- Colony count decreases ~10-fold with each 10-fold dilution in series.

### Marine Adaptation of iChip (Hagestad et al. 2020)

For seawater fungi on research cruises:
1. Collect seawater at depth in sterile Duran flask; store at 2°C if delay necessary.
2. Pour inoculated agar into in situ plate mold; cover with barrier membranes.
3. Incubate in seawater pool with constant seawater flow on deck.
4. Transfer visible mycelium to antibiotic-free agar once grown; subculture to axenic.

### Microbial Traps / Field-Deployable Device (Overy et al. 2019)

Simpler device; attach to driftwood, sea ice, or sediment:
- Stainless steel washer (~50 × 3 mm, 17 mm through-hole; 3 holes for securing).
- Bottom membrane: **0.4–0.6 µm polycarbonate** (large enough for hyphae to enter).
- Top membrane: smaller pore (blocks airborne contaminants).
- Fill with agar medium; glue membranes with aquarium silicone under laminar flow.
- Fungi grow in through bottom membrane; recover after incubation in situ.

### FiChip — Fungal iChip Adaptation (Li et al. 2023)

Specifically designed for fungi from mangrove sediments:
- 0.22 µm nylon membrane; 96 through-holes in polypropylene plate.
- Seawater agar inoculated with 50× diluted sediment suspension.
- 1-month in situ incubation in mangrove sediment.
- Result: 700+ fungal strains; 70% isolated only by iChip; 45 potential novel species.

### Hollow-Fiber Membrane Chamber (HFMC) — Aoi et al. 2009

Alternative to iChip for liquid-environment incubation:
- PVDF hollow-fiber membrane, 0.1 µm pore size, 0.76 mm inner diameter,
  1.2 mm outer diameter, 30 cm length per chamber.
- 48–96 chamber units; 130 µL volume each.
- Membrane immersed in natural liquid (river water, seawater, activated sludge);
  allows chemical exchange (nutrients, metabolites, signals) but not cell migration.
- Cells injected by syringe; incubate in situ environment; harvest and culture.
- Advantage over iChip: liquid medium (not agar), so amenable to flow-through
  environments and anaerobic liquid incubation.

### Miniaturized Culture Chip — Antarctic Bacteria (Palma Esposito et al. 2018)

Commercial closed-system alternative to iChip (Hoekmine BV, Utrecht).
Applied to Antarctic shallow-water sediment bacteria (*Aequorivita* sp.):
- Device mimics natural environment; captures slow-growing, previously unculturable taxa.
- Led to isolation of novel bacterium with antimicrobial + anthelmintic activity.
- Note: device was commercial, not DIY — relevant as precedent for cold-environment
  in situ cultivation without field deployment of custom device.

## VI. FUNGI vs. BACTERIA IN ICHIP — KEY DIFFERENCES

The iChip was designed for bacteria. Adaptations needed for fungi:

1. **Cell size:** Fungal spores (2–50 µm) and hyphae (2–10 µm wide) are far larger
   than bacteria (0.5–5 µm). Standard 0.03 µm membrane blocks fungal entry entirely.
   Use 0.05–0.22 µm for spore trapping; 0.4–0.6 µm for hyphal ingrowth traps.
2. **Single-cell loading:** Fungal spores can be loaded one-per-well by dilution, but
   hyphal fragments cannot be dispersed to single cells easily.
3. **Contamination:** Filamentous fungi spread across wells if membrane seal is
   imperfect. Each batch should include contamination-check wells.
4. **Domestication time:** Fungi may require longer domestication periods (months) than
   bacteria (weeks) before growing on conventional media.

**TIDAL Lab recommendation:** Start with the modified 96-well plate iChip
(Gauthier et al. 2025). Substitute SMS medium with seawater-based MEA or PDA.
Use 0.05 µm membrane for spore-based samples; 0.4–0.6 µm for hyphal-recovery traps.
Incubate membrane-down in a flow-through seawater tray if field deployment is
impractical; or embed in original sediment in a covered container.

## VII. EXTINCTION CULTURING / HTC (Connon & Giovannoni 2002)

High-throughput culturing using very-low-nutrient media:
- Dilute marine bacterioplankton (or water column fungi) to 1–10 cells/well in
  **200 µL** microtiter wells using natural, unamended seawater as the medium.
- Culture at in situ substrate concentrations (~3 orders of magnitude below
  lab media). Key principle: oligotrophic taxa cannot tolerate nutrient-rich media.
- Screen for growth: flow cytometry or OD detection in plate reader.
- Results: up to 14% of cells cultured vs. 0.01–0.1% by standard plating.
  Recovered 4 novel Proteobacteria clades (SAR11, OM43, SAR92, OM60).
- Application to fungi: dilution-to-extinction in 48-well or 96-well plates with
  natural seawater ± minimal nutrients recovers rare marine yeasts (Overy et al. 2014).

## VIII. THRAUSTOCHYTRIDS AND RELATED MARINE PROTISTS (Lyu et al. 2021; Hassett et al. 2021)

Thraustochytrids (Labyrinthulomycetes) are not true fungi but are ecologically and
methodologically related — frequently co-isolated and confused with fungi.

**Genera (culturable):** *Aplanochytrium, Aurantiochytrium, Botryochytrium,
Japonochytrium, Monorhizochytrium, Oblongichytrium, Parietichytrium, Schizochytrium,
Sicyoidochytrium, Thraustochytrium, Ulkenia, Labyrinthulochytrium*

**Isolation:**
1. **Pine pollen baiting (standard):** Add sterile pine pollen to seawater in petri dish;
   inoculate with sample. Thraustochytrids colonize pollen within days.
   Transfer colonized pollen to CMA or GPY seawater agar with antibiotics.
2. **Direct plating on highly nutritious agar** with penicillin + streptomycin.
3. **New method (Qarri et al.; Hassett et al. 2021):** Primary cell culture from
   colonial tunicate *Botryllus schlosseri* in liquid medium with basal medium,
   50% artificial seawater, vitamins, proteins, and antibiotics.
4. **Nile red screening:** Stain cells to visualize lipid droplets by fluorescence
   microscopy — selects for high-PUFA strains (*Schizochytrium, Thraustochytrium, Ulkenia*).

**Important:** Thraustochytrids are Stramenopiles (not fungi). They share flagellated
spores and fungal-like ectoplasmic nets but are phylogenetically closer to diatoms
and oomycetes. Use 18S not ITS for ID.

**Parasitic chytrids in algal culture (Carney & Lane 2014):**
- Chytrid contamination is one of the major causes of algal mass culture crashes
  (notably *Haematococcus pluvialis* for astaxanthin production).
- **Algal mass culture (AMC) method:** grow cultured host algae as bait for parasitic
  chytrids; Chytridiomycota and allied lineages infect host and can be isolated.
  Useful for obtaining obligate parasites that cannot be isolated without a host.

## IX. ADVANCED CULTUROMICS AND OMICS-GUIDED ISOLATION (Zhang et al. 2024)

Zhang et al. 2024 (Fungal Biology Reviews) is the best current review of
innovative cultivation strategies applicable to marine fungi. Key approaches:

### Culturomics (Multi-Condition High-Throughput Isolation)
- Apply many different media conditions + growth temperatures simultaneously.
- Combine with MALDI-TOF or ITS sequencing for rapid identification.
- Reduces per-condition effort; increases breadth of taxa recovered.
- Proven for gut microbiome; being extended to marine fungi.

### Metagenome-Guided Medium Optimization (Liu et al. 2022)
- Reconstruct MAGs from environmental metagenomes.
- Predict nutritional requirements (carbon sources, nitrogen, vitamins, oxygen)
  from gene content (metabolic pathway analysis via BlastKOALA/KEGG).
- Design media to match predicted requirements.
- Example: MAG-predicted starch utilization → starch-supplemented isolation medium
  recovered novel *Succinovibrionaceae* from wallaby gut.

### Reverse Genomics (Cross et al. 2019)
- Predict cell-surface proteins from MAG/SAG data.
- Engineer antibodies against predicted surface proteins.
- Use antibodies to capture target cells by FACS from complex community.
- Then cultivate captured cells.
- Demonstrated for human oral Saccharibacteria (TM7) — an obligate epibiont impossible
  to culture by traditional methods.
- Applicable in principle to any sequenced but uncultured organism.

### RACS — Stable-Isotope Probing + Raman Cell Sorting (reviewed in Zhang et al. 2024)
- Incubate sample with heavy water (D2O) and target substrate.
- Metabolically active cells incorporate D2O into lipids → distinctive C-D peaks
  in Raman spectrum (2040–2300 cm⁻¹).
- Sort labeled active cells with optical tweezers into sterile wells for cultivation.
- Advantage: culture-independent selection of physiologically active cells from a
  complex community — not just the fastest growers.

### EukRep — Eukaryotic Genome Recovery from Metagenomes (West et al. 2018)
- k-mer-based classifier: distinguishes eukaryotic from prokaryotic sequences
  in assembled metagenomes.
- Enables binning of fungal (and protist) genomes from complex environmental samples.
- Recovered near-complete genomes for Eurotiomycetes fungi from geyser community.
- Enables cultivation-independent metabolic inference for environmental fungi
  that cannot (yet) be grown in the lab.

### Cell Separation from Sediment for FACS Sorting (Morono et al. 2013)
- Multilayer density gradients: sodium polytungstate and/or Nycodenz.
- >80% cell recovery from shallow sediment (to 100 m depth);
  ~50% from deep samples (100–365 m).
- Flow cytometry with SYBR Green I: distinguishes cell-derived signal from
  SYBR-stainable particulate matter (SYBR-SPAM).
- Application: pre-sort environmental cells by FACS before cultivation attempts;
  enables single-cell isolation from sediments without standard colony formation.

### Droplet Microfluidics (Hu et al. 2021)
- Encapsulate single cells in nanoliter droplets (picoinjection + emulsification).
- Incubate in microfluidic device; screen droplets for growth by fluorescence.
- Sort positive droplets; extract cells for cultivation or sequencing.
- Advantage: high throughput (~10⁴–10⁶ droplets/run); separates competition;
  allows screening for specific phenotypes (enzyme production, antimicrobials).
- Not yet widely applied to marine fungi; primary literature is bacterial/archaea.

### Glass Bead Cultivation (Droce et al. 2013)
- Alternative to agar plates for studying secondary metabolite production.
- Glass beads as inert support + defined liquid medium → controlled nutrient supply.
- Advantages over agar: pure carbon control (agar contains trace elements/impurities);
  easier RNA/metabolite extraction than mycelia scraping from agar.
- Tested with *Fusarium* spp.: comparable secondary metabolite production to agar;
  superior to liquid shake culture.
- Use case: after isolating a marine fungus, use glass bead cultivation for
  controlled secondary metabolite profiling or gene expression studies.

## X. FREEZING AND LONG-TERM PRESERVATION

### Glycerol Freezing Protocol (Mitchison-Field & Gladfelter 2021)
1. Sub-culture isolate to axenic plate before freezing; check for bacteria by microscopy.
2. Mix 2× liquid isolation medium + 50% glycerol 1:1 → final: 1× medium, 25% glycerol.
3. Add 1 mL per cryovial (1.5 mL cryogenic vials, Fisher cat. no. 10-500-26).
4. Scrape small amount of colony with sterile pipette tip; add to vial. Cap tightly.
5. Vortex briefly (distribute cells evenly).
6. Flash freeze immediately in liquid nitrogen. Store at −80°C up to several years.

See also main skill file `preservation-storage.md` for lyophilization protocols.

## XI. MOLECULAR IDENTIFICATION

| Marker | Use | Primers |
|---|---|---|
| ITS (ITS1-5.8S-ITS2) | Universal first-pass, Ascomycota/Basidiomycota | ITS1/ITS4 or ITS5/ITS4 |
| ITS2 only (backup) | When full ITS amplification fails | ITS3/LR0Ri |
| D1-D2 of 28S | Marine yeasts, basidiomycetes, better resolution | NL1/NL4 |
| D1-D3 of 28S + 18S V1-V5 | Basal lineages, Chytridiomycota | LR0R/LR7 + NS1/NS4 |
| 18S (SSU) | Early-diverging fungi, environmental clone libraries | General SSU primers |
| β-tubulin | *Aspergillus*, *Penicillium* species | Bt2a/Bt2b |
| Actin | *Cladosporium* | ACT512F/ACT783R |
| Calmodulin | *Stachybotrys* | CL-1/CL-2 |

**Species cutoff:** 98% ITS similarity = standard species threshold (Hagestad et al. 2020).
Sequences below 98% similarity to closest GenBank match are treated as potentially novel.

**For marine fungi specifically:** 18S V1–V5 and 28S D1–D3 in addition to ITS
improve resolution for basidiomycetous yeasts and early-diverging lineages (Nagahama et al. 2011).
