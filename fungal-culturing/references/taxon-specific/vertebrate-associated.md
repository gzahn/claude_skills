# Fungi Associated with Vertebrates

> Source: Foster, Bills & Mueller (2004) Ch. 20 (Fungi Associated with Vertebrates — Summerbell).

---

## Overview

Vertebrate-associated fungi include: commensals (colonizing skin, mucous membranes, digestive tracts), pathogens (causing infections in various tissues), and environmental fungi isolated from vertebrate-influenced habitats (nests, dwellings, excretory deposits). Most mycotic disease agents have primary saprobic roles in nature; vertebrate infection is secondary.

**Important scope note:** Many saprobic fungi (especially thermotolerant ones) are also opportunistic pathogens. Their environmental isolation for biodiversity surveys uses standard techniques (see soil-fungi.md); this file focuses on techniques specific to vertebrate-associated isolation.

---

## Key Ecological Groups

| Group | Examples | Primary reservoir |
|---|---|---|
| Virulent systemic pathogens | *Blastomyces*, *Histoplasma*, *Coccidioides*, *Paracoccidioides* | Specific soils, bird/bat guano, plant debris |
| Lymphocutaneous pathogens | *Sporothrix schenckii* | Cellulosic plant debris (peat moss, grass stalks) |
| Dermatophytes | *Trichophyton*, *Microsporum*, *Epidermophyton*, *Arthroderma* | Soil (geophilic), animals (zoophilic), humans (anthropophilic) |
| Opportunistic pathogens | *Aspergillus fumigatus*, *Candida*, *Cryptococcus*, Mucorales | Compost, soil, decaying vegetation; commensal |
| Commensal yeasts | *Candida*, *Malassezia*, *Trichosporon* | Skin, mucous membranes, GI tract of mammals |
| Subcutaneous pathogens | *Sporothrix*, Herpotrichiellaceous anamorphs, *Pseudallescheria* | Soil, plant debris, thorns |
| *Pneumocystis carinii* | Cannot be cultured | Respiratory tract; contagious |

## Standard Media for Vertebrate-Associated Fungi

| Medium | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| SABCCG (Sabouraud + chloramphenicol + cycloheximide + gentamicin) | Primary isolation of dermatophytes, Onygenalean pathogens, Herpotrichiellaceous opportunists | Cycloheximide inhibits many saprobes; also some pathogens |
| SAB (without cycloheximide) | Oomycetes, cycloheximide-sensitive species | Use if Oomycetous infection is possible |
| Littman oxgall streptomycin | Systemic fungi; colony-growth restricting | See media-recipes.md |
| IMA (Inhibitory Mold Agar) | Systemic fungi; colony-growth restricting | See media-recipes.md |
| Blood agar + CCG + 2–3% egg albumen | Onygenalean pathogens from background of heavy *Candida* contamination | Egg white biotin-binds and inhibits most medical *Candida* |
| BHIA (Brain-Heart Infusion Agar) + CCG | High-protein medium for virulent Onygenalean pathogens | See media-recipes.md |
| BCP-MS-YE (Bromcresol purple–milk solids–yeast extract) | *Trichophyton verrucosum* | Distinctive clearing halo around colonies |
| Caffeic acid agar (Staib's) | *Cryptococcus neoformans* | Brown melanin pigment produced; specific reaction |
| SABC (SAB + cycloheximide) | Dermatophytes isolation | Standard dermatology use |
| Bandoni's sorbose-yeast-tetracycline medium | Body surface yeasts; restricts colony diameter | Prevents overgrowth of hyphomycetous contaminants |

**General systemic isolation standard set:**
1. SABCCG — selective for pathogens; inhibits many saprobes
2. Sheep blood CCG agar, BHIA-CCG, or SABHI-CCG — high-protein medium for Onygenalean pathogens
3. Littman oxgall streptomycin or IMA — colony-growth restricting; permits most hyphomycetous fungi

**Incubation:** 28–32°C for homeotherm fungi (37°C may favor *Coccidioides* but can inhibit others); lower temperatures for fungi from poikilotherms (reptiles, amphibians, fish); plates retained at least 3 weeks (5 weeks if *Histoplasma* is possible).

## Isolation from Superficial Infections

**Skin surface swabbing (commensals/superficial pathogens):**
- Moisten sterile cotton swab with sterile water or physiological saline
- Draw across skin surface; streak onto SABC or BCP-MS-YE
- Incubate at 30–35°C (approximates skin surface temperature of homeotherms)
- Lower temperatures for poikilotherms

**Contact plates (quantitative):**
- Medium poured flush with plate rim but air space remains when lid closed
- Appressed directly to skin; covered; incubated
- Bandoni's sorbose medium recommended to restrict colony diameter

**Skin scrapings (dermatophytosis):**
- Collect infected epithelium at margins of skin lesion with sterile scalpel blade
- Collect on folded black paper
- Mount in 25% KOH/5% glycerol; heat 1 hour at 51–54°C to emulsify fats; examine microscopically
- Inoculate scrapings onto SABCCG (with + without additional antibiotics)
- *Trichophyton verrucosum* (slow-growing): use BCP-MS-YE

**Hair plucking (infected hair follicles):**
- Pluck basal "root" ends of hairs from infected lesions
- If hair too fragile to pluck: surface scrapings with scalpel blade
- In dense fur/hair: sterile toothbrush to loosen and collect flakes
- *Microsporum canis* lesions: visualize with Wood's light (UV filtered through cobalt glass) for fluorescence guidance

**Ear swabbing (otomycosis):**
- Sterile cotton swab to collect ear secretions; streak on SABC
- Verify with 10% KOH mount: filamentous fungi must be producing mycelium in ear to be considered pathogenic, not contaminant

**Corneal scrapings (mycotic keratitis):**
- After appropriate anesthesia; collect with sterile scalpel blade
- Culture on SAB; verify infection by fungal filaments in KOH or gram smear

## Isolation from Subcutaneous Infections

**Deep swabbing or aspiration:**
- Swab or aspirate pus from lesions; plate on systemic mycology media set
- For subcutaneous mycetoma: grains (sclerotium-like structures) emerge from sinus channels; collect grains at skin surface; swish through agar to remove surface contamination; plate directly or pulverize in sterile water then plate; for hard, dense grains (*Madurella*): briefly disinfect surface with 70% ethanol

**Biopsy tissue:**
- Ground in sterile glass tissue homogenizer in sterile distilled water; plate slurry on systemic media set
- Gold standard: histopathological confirmation that fungal elements penetrate tissue

## Isolation from Systemic Infections

**Standard plate culture:** All systemic samples (sputum, bronchoalveolar lavage, biopsy, urine, CSF, blood) plated on standard set (SABCCG + high-protein medium + colony-restricting medium).

**Blood culture (automated systems):** Bactec system (Becton-Dickinson) using bottles of special medium with radioactive carbon; detects CO₂ increase from aerobic respiration; fungal medium bottles available. More rapid and efficient than plate culture.

**Distinguishing systemic fungi from contaminants:**
1. Presence of morphologically compatible fungal structures in normally sterile body site (histopathology or direct KOH)
2. Repeated isolation from normally sterile body site (if no direct microscopy)
3. Repeated isolation (or single isolation of Aspergillus fumigatus with compatible symptoms) from immunologically influenced nonsterile site
4. Isolation of organism essentially never encountered as a contaminant
5. Confirmation by fluorescent antibody staining, specific antibody titer, direct antigen test, or molecular genetic detection

## Environmental Isolation of Medically Important Fungi

### Systemic Pathogens

**Coccidioides immitis and Sporothrix schenckii:**
- Plate serial dilutions of bulk material (soil, peat moss, pulverized plant material) on SABCCG or SABCCG with enhanced cycloheximide + antibacterials (for *S. schenckii*)
- Incubation: 37°C may favor *Coccidioides*; ≤32°C for *Sporothrix*

**Histoplasma capsulatum and Blastomyces dermatitidis (very difficult to isolate from environment):**
- Prepare slurry of bulk material (soil, dust, bird/bat guano) in physiological saline
- Inject into rodents with sufficient antibiotics to inhibit bacteria
- Sacrifice animals showing signs of disease immediately; plate liver and spleen portions on systemic isolation media set
- Sacrifice all remaining animals at 6 weeks; examine similarly
- *Blastomyces*: most readily recovered during cool, damp weather; often ephemeral in environment

**Cryptococcus neoformans:**
- Most efficiently detected on Staib's *Guizotia abyssinica* seed medium or caffeic acid medium (brown melanin pigment produced; specific)
- Also grows on Littman oxgall, SAB at 37°C

### Dermatophytes (Hair Baiting)

**Simple version (Vanbreuseghem 1949; Dawson & Gentles 1961):**
1. Cut autoclaved horse mane/tail hair into 1–3 cm lengths; place on Petri plate filled with test soil
2. Alternatively: child's hair (ideally <1 year old) cut into 1–3 cm lengths on surface of garden soil half-filling a glass Petri dish; autoclave at 20 lbs / 20 min to sterilize soil and hair; then seed with test material
3. Incubate up to 4 weeks at room temperature
4. Examine hairs under dissecting microscope for *Arthroderma* gymnothecia, pseudogymnotheecia, macroaleurioconidia, or tufts/strands of microaleurioconidia
5. Remove structures with fine needle; culture on SABCCG; OR remove hairs, cut into minute segments, plate/streak onto SABCCG

**Quantitative isolation from dwellings:** Swab surfaces with saline-moistened cotton swab; streak on SABCCG + 5-fluorocytosine for additional selectivity (Sugimoto et al. 1995).

**Feathers, carnivore dung, nest materials:** Incubate in moist chamber; examine for *Arthroderma* and anamorphs.

### Opportunistic Pathogens from Environmental Materials

- General growth medium (Littman oxgall streptomycin) at **37°C**: increases recovery of *Aspergillus fumigatus*, *Pseudallescheria boydii*, other thermotolerant opportunists
- **SABCCG + 0.04–0.10% cycloheximide**: facilitates isolation of Herpotrichiellaceous anamorphs (*Exophiala*, *Cladophialophora*)
- **Benomyl (10 µg/ml)**: selects for endomycetous and many basidiomycetous yeasts, Mucorales, Microascaceae/anamorphs, Pleosporaceae; prepare stock suspension in sterile distilled water; swirl into cooling medium just before pouring plates

### Air Sampling for Opportunists

- Volumetric sampler (e.g., Andersen sampler) preferred over gravitational sedimentation (small, respirable conidia of target species not recovered adequately by gravity)
- Incubate at 37°C for fungi of homeotherms
- Standard Andersen sampler procedure otherwise (see general air mycology literature)

## Herbarium/Culture Vouchers for Medically Important Fungi

- Prepare dried cultures: add ~10 drops commercial 37% formaldehyde to medium; seal plate tightly at room temperature for 24 hours (formaldehyde vapor kills cultures); demonstrate that attempted subcultures are uniformly nonviable → valid herbarium specimen
- Key culture collections: University of Alberta Microfungus Collection and Herbarium; CBS (Centraalbureau voor Schimmelcultures); ATCC

## Biohazard Cautions

**Biohazard class 3 fungi (requires specialized containment):**
- *Coccidioides immitis*, *Histoplasma capsulatum*, *Blastomyces dermatitidis*
- Easily aerosolized, respirable conidia; virulent; potentially severe laboratory-acquired infections
- International shipping requires permits and navigating complex regulatory process

**Biohazard class 2 fungi (special facilities and shipping protocols required):**
- Opportunistic aspergilli, yeasts, dermatophytes

**All work with pathogenic fungi from vertebrate sources requires:**
- Formal ethical sanction and explicit consent (for human samples)
- Compliance with national regulations for animal studies
- Understanding of shipping regulations for biological materials (see preservation-storage.md)
