# Deep-Sea Fungi — Collection, Isolation, and Culturing

> **Note:** This file is intentionally sparse — the field of deep-sea mycology is nascent and the 2004 source textbook (Foster, Bills & Mueller) has limited coverage of deep-sea-specific methodology. This file should be expanded with primary literature as the lab's deep-sea program develops.

---

## What Is Known from the 2004 Source Textbook

- Indigenous (obligate) marine fungi have been collected as deep as **5,315 m** depth (Kohlmeyer 1977)
- **Dissolved oxygen** is the primary limiting factor for marine fungal distribution; no growth observed at ≤0.30 ml O₂/L; growth occurred at 1.26 ml O₂/L (Kohlmeyer 1969a, from California coast minimum oxygen zone)
- Deep-sea records were mostly from wood panel experiments, not active deep-sea sampling
- Most "marine fungi" diversity work as of 2004 was from **intertidal and shallow subtidal zones** (particularly mangroves and salt marshes)

## Key Gaps to Address with Primary Literature

The following aspects are not covered (or inadequately covered) in the 2004 source and should be supplemented with papers from the post-2004 literature:

1. **Pressure adaptation:** Cultivation under elevated hydrostatic pressure (piezophilic/piezotolerant fungi)
2. **Temperature requirements:** Psychrophilic deep-sea isolates; many deep-sea environments are near 2°C
3. **Nutrition under oligotrophy:** Deep-sea is nutrient-limited; most standard lab media may be too rich
4. **Sampling methods specific to deep sea:**
   - Niskin bottles (water column)
   - Sediment corers (box corers, kasten cores, push cores)
   - ROV-based collection (direct manipulator grab, suction samplers)
   - Baited lander deployments
   - Colonization substrata (wood panels, chitin plates, bone material — deep-sea "whale falls")
5. **DNA-based community assessment:** Environmental DNA (eDNA) metabarcoding approaches (ITS, 18S) from deep-sea water/sediment — likely the most productive approach for initial diversity characterization

## Starting Points for Media (Extrapolation from Principles)

When attempting to culture deep-sea isolates, apply principles from marine fungal culturing:

| Consideration | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Salt base | Use seawater-based media (SA, SSA, KMV — see media-recipes.md) |
| Nutrient level | Start with **very dilute** formulations (1/10 BHI, SNA, or diluted versions of seawater-based media) |
| Temperature | Incubate at 4°C or close to in situ temperature for psychrophilic isolates |
| Agar concentration | Lower agar (0.5–1%) may improve recovery and colony morphology |
| Pressure | Ideally, initial enrichment/baiting under elevated pressure (requires pressurized equipment) |
| Antibacterials | Same cautions as for marine oomycetes — avoid agents toxic to target group |

**Specific media worth trying:**
- **Dilute Brain-Heart Infusion (1/10 BHI):** Low-nutrient; nearly transparent; minute growth easily visible with dissecting scope. See media-recipes.md.
- **Silica-gel medium:** Specifically designed for oligotrophic fungi. Very low nutrient. Requires careful preparation. See media-recipes.md.
- **Seawater agar (SA):** Standard marine fungal medium with antibiotics. See media-recipes.md.
- **KMV medium:** Dissolved in seawater; appropriate for zoosporic fungi and Chromista from aquatic habitats. See media-recipes.md.
