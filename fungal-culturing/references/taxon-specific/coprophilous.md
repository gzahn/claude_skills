# Coprophilous Fungi — Sampling, Isolation, and Culturing

> Source: Foster, Bills & Mueller (2004) Ch. 21 (Coprophilous Fungi — Krug, Benny & Keller).

---

## Overview

Coprophilous fungi grow on and are associated with animal dung (mainly mammalian, both herbivorous and carnivorous; some birds). Most spores germinate only after passage through the mammalian digestive tract (especially Ascomycetes). Key groups: Myxomycetes, Zygomycetes, Ascomycetes, Basidiomycetes, and Myxobacteria.

**Ecological succession on dung:**
- Days 1–7 (fresh dung): Zygomycetes first (5–14 days)
- Days 7–30: Ascomycetes (middle succession)
- Days 30–60+: Basidiomycetes; Myxomycetes much later

**Sampling bias notes:**
- Ruminant dung ≠ lagomorph dung ≠ carnivore dung (each has distinct mycobiota)
- Habitat of host is more important than substratum type per se
- pH of dung varies with stage of decay; most coprophilous fungi prefer ~pH 7.0
- Tropical dung decomposes faster — select for faster-sporulating species
- Dung devoured largely by scarabaeid beetles: poor source (mainly crust, no moisture)

## Dung Collection

**General:**
- Collect in **paper bags** — NEVER airtight containers (plastic bags, screw-cap jars) — anaerobic conditions cause rapid bacterial degradation
- Dry dung can be stored in airtight containers in a refrigerator for long periods
- Use **forceps and rubber gloves** — dung may harbor *Histoplasma capsulatum*, *Coccidioides immitis*, and other pathogens
- Open moist chamber cultures only under a laminar flow hood when working in endemic disease areas

**Best sources by fungal group:**

| Target group | Best dung sources | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Myxomycetes | Cattle, horses, bison, rabbits | Several days old; crusted outer surface; hardened interior; avoid freshly defecated soft feces |
| Zygomycetes | Small animals: rats, mice, bats, small lizards, frogs | Fresh, dark, shiny dung; avoid rough/whitish dung (nutrients leached) |
| Ascomycetes & Basidiomycetes | Wide range; cow, horse, rabbit, deer | Both fresh and aged; sample multiple ages; avoid beetle-devoured dung |

**Quantity:**
- Small animals (deer, rabbit): collect at least a handful of pellets
- Large animals (cow, horse): aim for a brown paper grocery bag (#3 or #5) of dung

**Sampling design:**
- Sample both spring and summer (different mycobiota); winter sampling also beneficial
- In tropics: sample both dry and wet seasons
- Sample multiple elevations in mountainous areas
- Include dung of animals from range of feeding habits (herbivore, carnivore)
- Sample cave deposits at multiple depths (layers represent multiple generations)

## Moist Chamber Technique (Primary Method for All Groups)

See Appendix I in source for full moist chamber construction details. Basic principle: place dung in humid chamber at ambient or controlled temperature; observe periodically for fruiting bodies.

**Examine chambers every few days for 6–8 weeks.**

**To increase species recovery:**
- Maintain some chambers at cooler temperatures (additional species with narrow temperature ranges)
- Add ammonia/ammonium hydroxide rather than water to a few chambers (different pH optimum species)
- Subject some chambers to wet/dry cycles (stimulates Basidiomycetes, certain Ascomycetes, some Myxomycetes)

## Myxomycetes — Isolation and Culturing

### Isolation

- Use moist chamber technique (primary method)
- When plasmodia are actively feeding on dung surface: transfer a thin slice of substratum with portion of plasmodium to water agar; feed with sterile finely ground uncooked oat flakes; allow plasmodium to spread over surface and eventually produce fruiting bodies
- Fresh spores from cultures can be sown on nutrient-poor agar (water agar or half-strength cornmeal agar) for spore-to-spore cultivation

### Culturing

**Media for spore-to-spore cultivation:**
- Water agar or half-strength cornmeal agar (Gray & Alexopoulos 1968)
- Oat-dung extract plain agar (ODA) — successfully used for *Perichaena depressa* (Keller & Eliasson 1992)

**Protocol:**
1. Crush single sporangium; spread released spores in small area on sterile plastic Petri dish (60 mm × 15 mm) with ~8 ml agar
2. Submerge some spores by slashing agar; leave others on surface
3. Circle area on reverse of plate with black marker
4. Add 3 drops dilute *E. coli* suspension (inoculum prepared by adding pure bacterium to 10 ml glass-distilled water)
5. For Trichiales and Stemonitales: flood agar surface with 2 ml sterile glass-distilled water (require free water in early life history)
6. For Physarales: dry agar surface (no free water added)
7. Incubate at 23°C in **total darkness**; Physarales: 12h dark/12h light cycle
8. Day 7: pipette off water (except thin layer to prevent desiccation); sprinkle pulverized rolled oats over agar surface; return to incubator; examine periodically for fruiting bodies

### Harvesting and Storage

- Raise lid on one side to let culture dry gradually — if dries too quickly, sporangia collapse; too slowly, filamentous fungi overgrow
- Harvest in timely fashion; watch for mites and collembola
- Observe at 50–100× for small fruiting bodies
- Cut around fruiting bodies with sharp scalpel; transfer to collection box
- Glue small pieces of dung bearing fruiting bodies to inside lid of matchbox; record collection number on both box parts
- Use needle-nosed forceps for delicate species

### Microscopy

- 400–1000× required for spore and capillitial ornamentation
- Calibrated ocular micrometer required for spore diameter measurement
- **Temporary water mounts:** Best routine choice; isolated air bubbles create "lens effect" enhancing surface markings
- **Permanent mounts:** Clear lactophenol (dissolves calcareous structures — test for CaCO₃ presence first)
- **Semipermanent:** Absolute ethanol wetting agent + 3% KOH + glycerol; ring with clear nail polish
- **For Physarales with CaCO₃:** Mount in water first to observe structural features before using lactophenol

## Zygomycetes — Isolation and Culturing

### Isolation

- Isolate from moist chamber cultures using **stainless steel minuten insect pins** mounted in wooden or metal vise-grip handles (for wet-spored species) or watchmaker's forceps (dry or wet spores)
- Sterilize forceps: flame → cool in 95% ethanol → quick reflame → cool → use
- Sterilize insect pins: briefly flame tip → cool in sterile agar
- Wet sporangium or spore drop adheres to cooled pin/forceps tip; transfer to agar medium

**Timing:** Mucorales appear after 4–5 days at room temperature (22–25°C). Start incubation Wednesday/Thursday to observe succession the following week.

**For cold-requiring species:** Initiate cultures a day or two earlier; incubate some samples at 18°C.

**Push down tall Mucorales sporophores daily** (sweep close to dung pellet with steel needle) until few are produced — this exposes slower-growing parasitic Zygomycetes (*Piptocephalis*, *Syncephalis*) and late arrivals (*Coemansia*).

### Media

**Nutrient-rich (initial isolation, rapid growth):**
- MEYE (Malt Extract-Yeast Extract Agar) — preferred; nearly clear, allows observation of early spore germination under dissecting scope
- Emerson's YpSs agar
- V8-juice agar
- PDA
- Leonian's medium + yeast extract (LYE)

**Nutrient-poor (observation of vegetative structures, zygospore formation):**
- Modified synthetic Mucor agar (MSMA)
- Wheat germ agar (WG)
- Cornmeal agar (CMA)
- Potato-carrot agar (PCA)
- Choanephora agar (CH)
- 0.2-strength YpSs agar

**Special cases:**
- *Pilobolus* (obligate coprophile): must use dung-extract agar (DEA) or hemin medium; transfer sporangia directly from sporophore; incubate at 37°C overnight; then place in lighted incubator or near window
- *Thermomucor* spp. (thermophilic): acidic PDA (APDA) enhances zygospore formation
- Psychrotolerant/psychrophilic species (*Chaetocladium*, *Phycomyces*, *Helicostylum*): grow/sporulate only at ≤20°C; include incubation at 18°C
- Obligate parasites (*Piptocephalis*, *Syncephalis*, *Dimargaris*): require host fungus (usually Mucorales; some need *Chaetomium*); use *Cokeromyces recurvatus* as standard host (dark, low-growing colony)

**General incubation:** Most Mucorales: 25°C. Psychrophilic spp.: 7–18°C. Avoid sealing plates with Parafilm (CO₂ accumulation inhibits sporulation).

### Storage

- Transfer to test tube slants (same or different medium); store in refrigerator
- V8-juice agar or nutrient-poor agars promote sporulation and are ideal for refrigerator storage
- Loose cap wrapped with Parafilm to prevent contamination
- Storage duration: 6–12 months for most Mucorales; *Basidiobolus* and *Conidiobolus*: transfer every 2–3 weeks
- Long-term: lyophilization in double-strength skim milk (or skim milk + sodium glutamate + honey/raffinose/trehalose; Berny & Hennebert 1991); OR freeze at –85°C in 50% glycerol cryovials

## Ascomycetes and Basidiomycetes — Isolation and Culturing

### Sampling Notes

- Examine field samples under dissecting microscope; remove fruiting bodies with dissecting needle/fine probe; mount in water for microscopy
- For Basidiomycetes on field collections: prepare spore prints; record macromorphological features while fresh; air-dry with piece of substratum; place in small specimen box

### Moist Chamber Incubation

- Observe every few days for 6–8 weeks
- Vary conditions across chambers (temperature, pH/ammonia, wet/dry cycling) to maximize species diversity
- Permanent vouchers: dried pieces of dung bearing fruiting bodies (≥12 per specimen) + all prepared slides

### Dilution-Plating Method (Warcup 1950; modified for dung)

Especially useful for Ascomycetes (isolates taxa not appearing in moist chambers):

1. Macerate small portion of dung by hand
2. Place in 100- or 250-ml beaker with 50–70% ethyl alcohol sufficient to cover it; leave ~5 minutes (eliminates fast-growing molds and Zygomycetes — **omit if isolation of Zygomycetes is desired**; Bills & Polishook found 4 minutes optimal)
3. Shake beaker to resuspend macerated dung
4. Add small portions of suspension in sequence to sterile Petri plates until suspension is exhausted
5. Pour semi-cooled agar medium (with antibiotics to reduce bacteria) over dung in plates; rotate carefully; allow to cool until almost solidified before pouring to avoid killing dormant spores
6. Examine as colonies appear; transfer to fresh agar plates

**Bills & Polishook modification:** Use DRBC (dichloran-rose bengal-chloramphenicol; 0.1 g chloramphenicol added after cooling); add ethyl-alcohol-treated dung directly to molten semi-cooled agar; agitate; pour directly into Petri plates.

### Culturing

**Media for Ascomycetes and Basidiomycetes:**
- Natural media: V8-juice agar, Weitzman Silva-Hutner (WSH), dung-extract agar
- Semi-synthetic: modified Leonian's medium
- Restrictive (slow-growing taxa, Basidiomycetes): DRBC with 2 µg/L chlortetracycline + 30 µg/L streptomycin + 2 µg/L benomyl (Hutchison & Summerbell 1990)
- Benomyl alone (5–20 ppm) effective for isolating Zygomycetes from natural substrata
- Czapek's agar (CZ) and dung-extract agar promote growth of some taxa
- Weak media (cornmeal dextrose agar) better for some species

**Culturing from fruiting bodies:**
- Remove fruiting body with sterilized needle/probe; roll in agar to remove adhering debris; OR briefly immerse in drop of household bleach (5.25% NaOCl)
- Alternatively: break open fruiting body against Petri plate; remove spores; transfer to clean agar
- For discoidal fruiting bodies and Basidiomycetes: stick piece to inside of Petri lid (spore-bearing layer down); spores drop to agar surface; transfer to clean plate

**Antibiotics for isolation:** Chlortetracycline HCl (0.05 mg/L) + streptomycin sulfate (0.1 mg/L) effective for cleaning bacterial contamination. Scott et al. (1993): 60 ppm each of chlortetracycline HCl + streptomycin sulfate + benzylpenicillin added to Leonian's medium.

**Spore germination enhancement:**
- Acetate, hydroxide, phenol, heat, or rumen fluid as stimulants
- Phenol derivatives with substitution at "para" position most effective (Furuya & Naito 1980)
- 10% mixed pancreatic enzymes buffered to pH of target animal's digestive tract (typically pH 6.8 with HPO₄²⁻/H₂PO₄⁻ buffer in 3:1 ratio); treat 20 minutes; streak on clean agar; incubate 24h at 37°C
- UV light can enhance fruiting body production and ostiole formation in pyrenomycetes
- Multiple incubation temperatures beneficial (some fungi have narrow temperature requirements)

### Microscopy for Identification

- Ascomycetes: asci and ascospores essential
- Basidiomycetes: basidia and basidiospores essential
- Use culture media that promote formation of reproductive structures
- Rich media (Leonian's, WSH): satisfactory for most
- Poorer media may reduce aerial hyphae for better microscopy
- Most Basidiomycetes: identification possible from fruiting bodies produced in moist chambers
