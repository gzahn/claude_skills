# Fungi in Stressful / Extreme Environments

> Source: Foster, Bills & Mueller (2004) Ch. 14 (Fungi in Stressful Environments — Zak & Wildman).

---

## Overview

"Extreme environments" are those in which temperature, pH, water activity, or concentrations of salts/nutrients are outside normal ranges. Fungi adapted to these conditions (extremophiles) are not "stressed" by them — standard isolation conditions (nutrient-rich media at room temperature) are the stressful conditions for an extremophile. **The key to isolating extremophiles is replicating the environmental conditions from which they came.**

---

## I. Thermotolerant and Thermophilic Fungi

**Definitions:**
- **Thermophilic:** grow only at temperatures from above 20°C to above 50°C
- **Thermotolerant:** grow below 20°C but also at 40–50°C or more
- ~70 species total; ~12 cosmopolitan species commonly isolated from soils

**Habitats:** Compost piles, mushroom beds, municipal waste/compost deposits, wood chip piles, bird nests, alligator nests, coal tips, volcanic hot springs. Ubiquitous as propagules in most soils.

**Common thermophilic taxa:** *Rhizomucor pusillus*, *R. miehei*, *Thermomyces lanuginosus*, *Talaromyces thermophilus*, *T. emersonii*, *Thermoascus aurantiacus*, *Chaetomium thermophilum* vars., *Scytalidium thermophilum*, *Thielavia terrestris*, *Malbranchea cinnamomea*, *Melanocarpus albomyces*. (Full table of 24 widespread species with habitats in source text.)

**⚠ Safety note:** Fungi from high-temperature sites can be serious human pathogens. Use appropriate safety measures.

### Collection

- Compost, wood chip piles, bird/alligator nests, thermal springs, heated soils
- Collect in sterile bags; process promptly

### Isolation Protocol

1. **Incubate primary cultures at 45–50°C** — never below 40°C
2. Primary transfer plates: start at 50°C; move to 40°C after good growth (prevents agar from drying)
3. Prevent moisture loss at 50°C: place plates in sealed containers lined with moist paper towels; OR seal plates with Parafilm (watch for condensation)
4. **PDA is unsatisfactory** — shrivels along edges at 50°C; use Emerson YS agar or other stable media

**Common isolation media:** Emerson Yeast Starch Agar (+ rose bengal + gentamicin + penicillin G — see media-recipes.md), Oatmeal agar I, Potato carrot agar, V8-juice agar.

### Selective Enrichment for Rare Thermophilic Species (Tansey & Jack 1976)

Add one of seven carbon sources to agar on 1% w/w basis: cellulose powder, carnauba wax, unbleached chitin, mixed keratin, soluble starch, lactose + gentamicin (100 mg/g soil), or lignin. Incubate bottles of soil + enriched carbon source for 12–25 days at 50°C; keep moist (add sterile water periodically). Prepare pour-plates and incubate at 50°C.

**Killing dominant spore-formers to reveal rare species:**
- Method A: Soak 15 g soil in 70% ethanol for 10 minutes; wash with sterile water; then plate (ethanol pasteurization)
- Method B: Soak 1 g soil in 5.25% sodium hypochlorite for 5 minutes; wash by repeated centrifugation and resuspension in sterile water; then plate

## II. Psychrotolerant and Psychrophilic Fungi

**Definitions:**
- **Psychrophilic:** optimum growth ≤16°C; maximum growth 20°C; can grow at 0°C; restricted to permanently cold habitats
- **Psychrotolerant (psychrotropic):** maximum growth above 20°C but can grow at and below 10°C

**Habitats:** Deep sea (90% of marine water volume is below 5°C), polar soils, alpine soils/lakes, snow and ice fields, glaciers, cold storage environments, food products.

**Common cold-adapted genera:** *Alternaria*, *Cladosporium*, *Keratinomyces*, *Leptomitus*, *Mucor*, *Mortierella*, *Penicillium*, *Rhizopus*, *Typhula*, *Cryptococcus albidus* (Antarctica), *Heteroconium chaetospira*, *Leptodontidium obscurum*.

**Snow molds (cause disease under snow cover):** *Coprinus psychromorbidus*, *Microdochium nivale*, *Myriosclerotinia borealis*, *Typhula ishikariensis*.

**Key ecological note:** Psychrophilic fungi coexist with mesophiles in soils and litter — their spores survive warm periods. Incubating the same soil at 0°C vs. 25°C produces **completely different communities** (Carreiro & Koske 1992 found Mortierella and Mucor spp. dominated at 0°C; mitosporic ascomycetes at 25°C).

### Isolation Protocol

**Method (Carreiro & Koske 1992):**
1. Plate forest soil/litter **directly onto chilled MYP agar** (Malt-Yeast-Peptone; see media-recipes.md) OR
2. Dilute sample and make pour-plates using agar cooled to 43°C
3. Incubate at **0°C** and examine twice weekly for up to 4 weeks

**Method (Petrini et al. 1992b for glacier/snowfield fungi):**
1. Incubate samples for **10 weeks at 4°C**
2. Transfer to 2% malt-extract agar slants; incubate again at 4°C
3. Continue until substantial growth observed (3–10 more weeks)

**Confirming psychrophily vs. psychrotolerance:** After isolation, incubate isolates at temperatures above 20°C — psychrophiles will not grow; psychrotolerant fungi will.

## III. Xerotolerant and Xerophilic Fungi

**Definitions:**
- **Xerophilic:** can grow at water activity (aW) below 0.85 under at least one set of conditions (Pitt 1975)
- **Xerotolerant:** tolerates low moisture but not strictly xerophilic
- Most xerophilic fungi are ascomycetes or their anamorphs

**Key taxa:** *Aspergillus* spp. (most xerotolerant in general), *Penicillium* spp. (more common in temperate climates), *Chrysosporium*, *Wallemia*, *Xeromyces*, *Eremascus*, *Paecilomyces*.

**Habitats:** Arid/semi-arid ecosystems, stored cereal grains, dried foods, food products with low water activity.

**Water activity reference (aW tables from source):**

| Solute | Molality for aW 0.98 | for aW 0.95 | for aW 0.90 | for aW 0.85 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Glycerol | 1.11 | 3.32 | 5.57 | 8.47 mol/kg |
| NaCl | 0.607 | 1.77 | 2.83 | 4.03 mol/kg |
| Sucrose | 1.03 | 2.72 | 4.11 | 5.98 mol/kg |

**Glycerol amounts to achieve target water potentials at 25°C:**

| Water potential (MPa) | Glycerol (g/L) |
|---|---|
| –0.5 | 8.36 |
| –1.0 | 19.02 |
| –2.0 | 35.59 |
| –3.0 | 54.28 |
| –5.0 | 91.08 |

### Isolation Protocol

- **Recommended medium:** DG18 (Dichloran-Glycerol-18; final aW 0.955) — best for xerophilic fungi from low-moisture foods and arid environments (Hocking & Pitt 1980). See media-recipes.md.
- Adjust diluent: use 20–30% glucose or fructose in peptone water to prevent osmotic shock to diluted samples (Corry 1987)
- Glycerol is preferred solute over NaCl (NaCl at high concentrations may be toxic to some target fungi)
- Growth rates change at different aW values depending on temperature and pH — run pilot experiments

## IV. Osmophilic Fungi

**Definition:** Grow on substrata with low water activity resulting from osmotic effects (high sugar or salt concentration). Overlaps with halophilic group; distinguished by osmotic vs. matric mechanism.

**Key taxa:** *Aspergillus, Aureobasidium, Chrysosporium, Cladosporium, Fusarium, Wallemia, Xeromyces*; osmophilic yeasts: *Debaryomyces hansenii*, *Saccharomyces rouxii*.

**Substrata:** Salted fish, jams, jellies, candy; naturally saline soils.

**Isolation:** Adjust aw of media with NaCl, sugars, glycerol, or polyethylene glycols. Use 40–60% sucrose Czapek's agar for very osmophilic fungi from desert soils. Prevent osmotic shock by keeping diluent at similar aW to target medium.

## V. Halotolerant and Halophilic Fungi

**Definitions:**
- **Halophilic:** require NaCl; extreme halophiles grow best at 2.5–5.2 M NaCl; borderline extreme at 1.5–4.0 M NaCl
- **Halotolerant:** tolerate salt to saturation but do not require it
- **No extreme halophiles** have been found among eukaryotes

**Key taxa:** *Basipetospora halophila*, *Hortaea werneckii*, *Aspergillus penicilloides* (optimum: 2.5 M NaCl); also *Alternaria*, *Aspergillus*, *Penicillium*, *Myrothecium*, *Stachybotrys*, *Trichoderma* (halotolerant from arid soils).

**Habitats:** Salt marshes, saline soils, seawater, salted/dried fish, brine-preserved foods.

### Isolation Protocol

1. Modify standard isolation media by adding **Instant Ocean** (artificial seawater salt; Carolina Biological Supply) or **sea salts** (Sigma)
2. Adjust NaCl concentration to match habitat: seawater = ~0.5 mol/kg (~3.0%); Great Salt Lake = 105.4 g Na⁺/liter
3. **Media:** Halophile agar (see media-recipes.md): malt extract + yeast extract + rose bengal + NaCl 150–200 g/L; MY5-12 agar (aW 0.96) or MY10-12 agar (aW 0.93)
4. Add antibacterial antibiotics after autoclaving
5. Incubate in dark at 28°C for 6–8 weeks

**Alternative for desert halophiles (Abdel-Hafez et al. 1989):** Glucose-substituted Czapek-Dox agar + 15–20% NaCl/liter.

## VI. Acidophilic and Alkalophilic Fungi

**Acidophilic habitats (pH ≤ 3–4):** Hot springs, coal mine refuse, drainage waters, mining effluents.

**Alkalophilic habitats (pH 8–11):** Alkaline soda lakes/deserts, concrete surfaces, cement/electroplating/paper manufacturing sites, animal excreta in soil.

**Key alkalophilic taxa:** *Aspergillus oryzae* (pH 9–9.5), *Exophiala alcalophila* (pH 10.4 in soil), *Phaeococcomyces alcalophilus*, *Acremonium* spp., *Fusarium* spp. (~30% of fungi from limestone caves are alkaline-tolerant or alkalophilic).

### Isolation Protocol

- **Adjust pH after autoclaving** (autoclaving at extreme pH can destroy agar matrix or change color/texture; may need to increase agar concentration)
- **Acidic media:** Standard media (PDA, malt, cornmeal agar) acidified with tartaric, lactic, citric, phosphoric, or hydrochloric acids
- **Alkaline media:** Add sodium carbonate, sodium bicarbonate, potassium carbonate, or sodium borate (0.5–2% salt concentration; target pH 8.5–11). Use **Horikoshi I** (glucose-based) or **Horikoshi II** (starch-based) media. Alkaline cornmeal agar (ACMA) also effective. See media-recipes.md.
- Otherwise use standard soil isolation methods (Ch. 13 protocols)

## VII. Oligotrophic Fungi

**Definition:** Fungi that grow under very low nutrient concentrations; found in nutrient-poor environments (deep sea, clean water, rock surfaces).

### Isolation Protocol

1. Use **minimal carbon media** or complete **silica-gel medium** lacking a carbon source (Parkinson et al. 1989; see media-recipes.md)
2. **Trace-contamination-free glassware required:** Wash all glass in 5% hydrochloric acid; heat in muffle furnace at 500°C to eliminate all traces of organic compounds (also heat foil and silica gel at 500°C for 1–2 hours)
3. Use glass (not plastic) Petri dishes
4. Prepare all media with extreme care to exclude contamination

## VIII. Rock-Inhabiting Fungi

**Habitats:** Rock surfaces (lichens, free-living), interior of rocks (endolithic fungi), calcareous substrata, subaerial biofilms.

**Key taxa:** Black meristematic fungi (Herpotrichiellaceae), dematiaceous hyphomycetes.

### Isolation Protocol

1. Dissect surface or interior rock samples; place on DRBC or 1:50 diluted Czapek-Dox agar
2. Alternatively: wash samples using soil-washing/particle-filtration methods (Ch. 13) to remove spores before plating
3. DRBC inhibitors and the oligotrophic conditions of diluted Czapek-Dox are necessary for slow-growing taxa
4. **Identification resources:** Ellis (1971, 1976) *Dematiaceous Hyphomycetes* and *More Dematiaceous Hyphomycetes*; de Hoog (1999) for physiological and rDNA-based phylogenetic analyses

## IX. Phoenicoid Fungi (Post-Fire)

**Definition:** Fungi that preferentially appear or fruit after fire; some are obligate fire-followers, others are opportunists exploiting post-fire nutrient flush and reduced competition.

**Fruiting may persist 1–4 years after a fire.** Common phoenix genera include various Pezizales (cup fungi), *Pyronema*, morels (*Morchella*), and various basidiomycetes.

### Collection

- **Macrofungi:** Collect sporocarps as per Ch. 8 macrofungi protocols; maintain collecting schedule for up to **4 years** post-burn
- **Microfungi:** Incubate post-fire soil samples and burned plant litter in Petri dishes in lab; place sterile straw or filter paper on soil to induce fruiting; keep moist but not saturated; fruiting begins 1 week post-incubation and continues up to 50 days

### Key taxa references

Ramsbottom (1953), Petersen (1970, 1971), Carpenter et al. (1987), Ellis & Ellis (1988).

## Summary: Medium / Temperature Matrix

| Group | Primary medium | Incubation temp | Incubation duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thermophilic | Emerson YS agar; Oatmeal agar I | 45–50°C (never <40°C) | 1–4 weeks |
| Psychrophilic | MYP agar; MEA 2% | 0–4°C | 3–10 weeks |
| Xerophilic | DG18 (aW 0.955) | Room temperature | 2–4 weeks |
| Osmophilic | Czapek-Dox + 40–60% sucrose; DG18 | Room temperature | 2–6 weeks |
| Halophilic | Halophile agar; MY5-12; MY10-12 | 28°C | 6–8 weeks (in dark) |

| Alkalophilic | Horikoshi I or II; ACMA | Habitat-appropriate | Varies |
| Oligotrophic | Silica-gel medium; minimal carbon agar | Habitat-appropriate | Extended |
| Rock-inhabiting | DRBC; dilute Czapek-Dox (1:50) | Room temperature | 4–8 weeks |
| Phoenicoid | Standard soil media (Ch. 13); moist chambers | Room temperature | 1–50 days post-setup |
